# The City of Eight — the subtle body (puryaṣṭaka)

Yes — Abhinavagupta explicitly equates the body with the City of Eight (puryaṣṭaka) and defines body-ness (dehatva) as the 'I'-sense (ahaṃtā / ahaṃbhāva): to 'burn the body' is to destroy that I-sense, and that is liberation. The clearest full-stack witness is Tantrasāra 13.24; Tantrāloka 15.234–236 states the same equation, and Tantrāloka 24.20–21 says that with the puryaṣṭaka gone there is no heaven or hell — i.e. it is the transmigrating subtle vehicle. Tantrāloka 9.204–205 ties it to the six kañcukas that contract the knower (the aṇu).

## [T] The body IS the City of Eight, and its body-ness is the 'I'-sense; burning the body means destroying that I-sense.
### Tantrasāra 13.24 — 13.24
> evaṃ sthite uttarābhimukham upaviśya dehapuryaṣṭakādau ahambhāvatyāgena dehatāṃ dahet / saṃnidhāv api paradehavat adehatvāt tato nistaraṅgadhruvadhāmarūḍhasya dṛṣṭisvābhāvyāt yā kila ādyā spandakalā saiva mūrtiḥ

Thus standing, facing north one should sit, burning body-ness in body-city-eight and the rest by I-feeling abandonment; from nearness as another's body, from bodilessness — then mounted on the waveless steady abode, from gaze-nature — which indeed is the first throb-digit — that alone is the form.

### Tantrāloka 15.234–236 — 15.234–236
> tena mantrāgninā dāho dehe puryaṣṭake tathā / dehapuryaṣṭakāhantāvidhvaṃsādeva jāyate / nahi sadbhāvamātreṇa deho@sāvanyadehavat / ahantāyāṃ hi dehatvaṃ sā dhvastā taddaheddhruvam

By that mantra-fire the burning is in the body, the city of eight; liberation arises precisely from destroying the I-ness of the body-city-of-eight. For it is not a body by mere existence, like another body; body-ness lies in the I-sense — that, destroyed, is verily burnt.

## [T] The City of Eight is the transmigrating vehicle: with it gone, heaven and hell and the rest do not obtain.
### Tantrāloka 24.20–21 — 24.20–21
> eṣā sāṃnyāsikī dīkṣā puryaṣṭakaviśodhanī / puryaṣṭakasyābhāve ca na svarganarakādayaḥ

This is the renunciate initiation, purifying the city-of-eight; and with the puryaṣṭaka gone, no heaven, hell, and the rest.

## [T] The six kañcukas contract the knower situated 'in the body, the city of eight, and the rest'; that contracted knower is the aṇu.
### Tantrāloka 9.204–205 — 9.204–205
> māyā kalā rāgavidye kālo niyatireva ca / kañcukāni ṣaḍuktāni saṃvidastatsthitau paśuḥ / dehapuryaṣṭakādyeṣu vedyeṣu kila vedanam / etatṣaṭkasasaṃkocaṃ yadavedyamasāvaṇuḥ

Māyā, kalā, rāga, vidyā, kāla and niyati — these six are called the coverings; in their presence the knower is the bound one (paśu). Knowing occurs, indeed, in the knowables beginning with the body, the city of eight; that contraction by this sixfold set is what is unknown to him — he is the atom (aṇu).

## [R] The eight constituents are the five tanmātras (sound, touch, form, taste, smell) together with manas, ahaṃkāra and buddhi.
### Tantrāloka 24.18–19 — 24.18–19
> pūjayitvā śrutisparśau rasaṃ gandhaṃ vapurdvayam / dhyahaṃkṛtī manaśceti brahmādiṣvarpayetkramāt

Having worshipped sound, touch, taste, smell, the two forms, and the I-maker and mind, one should offer them in order to Brahmā and the rest.
