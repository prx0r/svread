## 1. Tier A — directly load-bearing for this estate

### ch. 31 — Ben Williams, “Cosmogenesis and Phonematic Emanation” (pp. 787–810)

The single best orientation in English to the **word → resonance → phoneme → world**
complex that the Mālinī/Mātṛkā frontier is built on. If one chapter is read end-to-end,
this one. Its value is not a new thesis but a *diachronic, inter-lineage map with primary
citations* — exactly what a translation-frontier bibliography needs.

Argument in one line: tantric cosmologies of language inherit Vedic Vāc/OM and
Bhartṛhari’s Word-absolute, then diverge on **where the phonemes sit ontologically** —
the dualistic Śaiva Siddhānta pushes the subtlest sound *outside* Śiva and the self
(nāda-vāda), while nondual Śākta-Śaiva (Trika, Abhinavagupta) keeps the phonemes
*inside* consciousness itself (varṇa-vāda).

Section-by-section leads (all editions named in the chapter’s bibliography, pp. 800–810):

1. **Precedents.** Ṛgvedic Vāc (Jamison & Brereton 2014); OM as sonic absolute;
   Bhartṛhari’s `śabda-brahma`, `sphoṭa` and the levels of speech (`paśyantī` /
   `madhyamā` / `vaikharī`), which every later tantric system reuses.
2. **Niśvāsa corpus** (Goodall, Sanderson & Isaacson 2015 — *the* critical edition of
   the earliest Śaiva tantra). `nāda` from formless Śiva; `tantrāvatāra` (descent of
   scripture); the dictum “all deities have mantra as their nature…”; `vāgeśī` as
   initiatory mother.
   - `Uttarasūtra`: Mātṛkā = the syllabary in eight letter-groups, a `vidyā`, source of
     all words/scriptures/mantras/languages; ritual worship placing vowels and
     consonants on the body’s left/right.
   - Etymology: tradition glosses `mātṛkā` as “mother/matrix”; Törzsök (2016) suggests
     an original link to `mātrā`, the prosodic mora — useful for the reader’s
     phoneme/prosody layer.
   - `Nayasūtra`: yoga of *shaping the body into letter-forms*, phoneme↔tattva
     homology, explicit soteriology (“who recognises body = letters = tattvas is
     liberated”); introduction of **Gupta-script graphemes** as a visual medium.
   - `Guhyasūtra` / `Niśvāsakārikā`: Mātṛkā becomes an independent goddess and is
     identified with **kuṇḍalinī**.
3. **Śaiva Siddhānta** (relative chronology after Goodall 1998).
   - `Svāyaṃbhuvasūtrasaṅgraha`: Śiva as **Śabdarāśi**, the mass of letters; 16 vowels
     = `bīja`, 34 consonants = `yoni`.
   - `Sarvajñānottara`: `nāda` → `bindu` → `śabda` → phonemes → beings.
   - `Kiraṇatantra` `mātṛkotpatti`: Mātṛkā explains how one body of knowledge becomes
     tarka, kāvya, itihāsa, scripture, regional song.
   - `Sārdhatriśatikālottara`: nāda in three grades matching the three levels of
     speech; kuṇḍalinī in the heart, three channels, nectar.
   - `Mṛgendra` verse: `śakti → nāda → bindu → akṣara → mātṛkā` (glossed by
     Nārāyaṇakaṇṭha; `śakti` = kuṇḍalinī = `parāvāc`).
   - `Parākhyatantra`: `bindu` as material cause, Śiva as efficient; a debate with
     Bhartṛhari’s `sphoṭa` that ends by denying any sphoṭa apart from the phonemes.
   - **Śrīkaṇṭha, `Ratnatrayaparīkṣā`**: the “standard series”
     `bindu/kuṇḍalinī → nāda → bindu → śabdarāśi/arṇa` (Sferra 2007), plus four
     `bindu` levels matching the four speech levels — and a *dualistic* ontology in
     which language is finally insentient.
   - **Rāmakaṇṭha, `Nādakārikā`**: nāda-vāda, i.e. nāda does the semantic synthesis;
     answers Kumārila’s `varṇavāda`; makes nāda the functional equivalent of sphoṭa.
   - Chapter’s sharp claim: **the mature commentarial emanation-schema may have no
     single scriptural source** (Goodall 2001, 328) — a caution for anyone trying to
     map “the” tantric sequence.
4. **Non-Saiddhāntika Śaiva / Śākta.**
   - `Svacchandabhairava`: eight mother-goddesses over letter-groups.
   - `Brahmayāmalatantra` (Hatley 2018): 50 phonemes as mantra-deities around
     Kapālīśabhairava; `avadhūtā śakti`/`icchā-śakti` as kuṇḍalinī; kuṇḍalinī
     visualised as the **loop of the grapheme A**; phonemes inlaid in inner cakras;
     Mālinī as a honeycomb, creation by `kṣobha` (shaking the hive).
   - `Siddhayogeśvarīmata`: kuṇḍalinī as `jagadyoni`, source of phonemes.
   - **Mālinī decoding** (Vasudeva 2004, 2007): the “scrambled” NA→PHA order is not
     scrambled — the sequence follows the **Gupta-Brāhmī letter shapes making up the
     goddess’s body** from topknot (NA) to left foot (PHA). `nyāsa` installs each
     phoneme on its body part; the result is a “synaesthetic icon.” This is the
     key to the wheel/body-visualisation lane.
   - `Vāmakeśvarīmata`: Tripurasundarī heralded as Mātṛkā, “thread on which the three
     worlds are strung”; vowel **E** = inverted triangle = centre of the Śrīcakra;
     `mahābindu`/`kāmakalā` and the three dots of Ī (seed `ĪṂ`).
   - **`Parātrīśikā`**: to Williams’s knowledge, the **earliest fully explicit
     phonematic cosmogenesis** — `sṛṣṭikrama` mapping vowels to `tithi`, consonants
     to `tattva`; vowels as the ur-movement *inside* `śiva-tattva`; the vowel/consonant
     boundary is the threshold of creation. Expanded in Abhinavagupta’s `Parātrīśikā-
     vivaraṇa`, `Tantrāloka` 3, `Tantrasāra` (see Padoux 1990, 233–304; Bäumer 2011,
     146–157).
   - `Śivasūtra` + Kṣemarāja: Mātṛkā as the power that *obscures* by generating
     limited, language-conditioned identity; when recognised, the same Mātṛkā
     liberates. Gloss: the “unknown mother” (`a-jñāta` + `mātṛkā` reading).
   - **Abhinavagupta**: `śāmbhavopāya`; phonematic emanation as the self-
     differentiation of awareness; the goal is recognising the whole syllabary as
     one’s own freedom; **AHAM = A (Śiva) + HA (Śakti)**, the first-person pronoun as
     the union of first and last phoneme. Against Rāmakaṇṭha’s nāda-vāda,
     Abhinavagupta sides with the Mīmāṃsaka `varṇavāda`: phonemes are irreducible and
     are “not a content of consciousness but consciousness itself” (Torella 2004,
     178–179).
5. **Stated desiderata** (p. 798–9): (a) a systematic study of **kuṇḍalinī in classical
   tantric sources**; (b) the rise of **script/graphemes** as a technology that changed
   ritual worship and tantric yoga. Both are live for us.

**Concrete uses for svread**
- Source of a defensive bibliography for the frontier packet: Niśvāsa, Mālinīvijayottara,
  Brahmayāmala, Kiraṇa, Parākhya, Nādakārikā, Parātrīśikā, Vāmakeśvarīmata.
- Justifies the reader’s **phoneme → body locus → Śakti → iconography** chain
  (Vasudeva’s Mālinī decoding is the missing link between letter order and body order).
- Gives the correct *comparative* frame: a schema is Saiddhāntika (nāda-vāda) or
  nondual Śākta (varṇa-vāda) — do not blend them silently in a translation note.
- Names the primary text for the “body shaped as letters” practice: `Nayasūtra`.
- Warns that `mātṛkā` is polysemic (syllabary / goddess / obscuring power / mother);
  the reader should not collapse these into one gloss.

### ch. 8 — Sthaneshwar Timalsina, “Cosmic Process, Philosophy, and Soteriology in Abhinavagupta” (177–196)
Compact orientation to Abhinavagupta as philosopher of `svātantrya`, reading across
`ĪPV`, `Tantrāloka`, `Paramārthasāra`, `Tantrasāra`, `PTV`, `Mālinīvijayavārttika`.
Use for framing/definitions in essays about recognition and freedom; do not treat as a
translation source.

### ch. 9 — Glen A. Hayes, “Esoteric Physiology and Subtle Body Systems” (197–224)
Survey of `yogic/subtle body` constructions across Hindu and Buddhist tantra, with the
explicit warning that there are *many* incompatible systems (no single “the subtle
body”). Directly relevant to deitybody/wheels: use it to keep our body-maps labelled by
lineage and text, not generic. Ends by reviewing cognitive-science/neuroscience
approaches.

### ch. 29 — Timalsina, “Attention, Memory, and the Imagination: A Cognitive Analysis of Tantric Visualization” (731–754)
`mantra` / `maṇḍala` / `mūrti` read through attention, memory, imagination; explicit
limits of importing cognitive science into tantric philosophy. Useful for the
visualisation/practice UI and for wording any cognitive claim.

### ch. 40 — Dominic Goodall, “Śaiva Tantra: Toward a History” (1035–1056)
The authority-grade short history of the formative Śaiva period and the Śaivasiddhānta’s
rise; a gateway to Sanderson’s articles. Use for chronology and for “what can we date”
decisions in `data/biblio.authority.json`-style work — never for verse translation.

### ch. 16 — David Gordon White, “Three Shades of Tantric Yoga: Chapter 20 of the Netra Tantra” (369–394)
NT 20’s `trividha yoga`: `parayoga` ≈ yoginī consorting (`haṭhamelaka`), `sūkṣmayoga`
≈ `parakāyapraveśa`, `gross yoga` = sorcery/countersorcery; “yoga” here means
*yoking/possession*. High value for any claim that “tantric yoga = meditation”: it is a
corrective. Also a candidate translation target with a clear, compact chapter.

### ch. 14 — Anna A. Golovkova, “Śrīvidyā and Goddess Traditions” (323–346)
Critical historiography of the study of Śrīvidyā/Tripurasundarī; useful for
Śrīcakra/goddess material and for avoiding repeated Orientalist/Woodroffe-era framings.

### ch. 1 — Payne & Hayes, “Tantric Studies: Issues, Methods, and Scholarly
Collaborations” (1–28)
Definition/category/origins problem, and the volume’s topical (not sectarian) rationale.
Best single citation for “tantra is a category, not a thing” in site copy.

## 4. Primary-source leads to acquire or verify (harvested from ch. 31)

Buy/verify these before the next frontier sprint; all are cited in the chapter, most
with the critical edition named.

- `Niśvāsatattvasaṃhitā`, Mūla/Uttara/Naya-sūtras, ed./tr. Goodall, Sanderson &
  Isaacson (2015) — **the** earliest Śaiva layer; Mātṛkā, `nāda`, `tantrāvatāra`.
- `Mālinīvijayottaratantra`, chs. 1–4, 7, 11–17, tr. Vasudeva (2004).
- Vasudeva, “Synaesthetic Iconography: 1. The Nādiphāntakrama” (2007) — the Mālinī
  body-as-letters decoding. **Priority read.**
- `Brahmayāmalatantra` I (chs. 1–2, 39–40, 83), ed./tr. Hatley (2018).
- `Kiraṇatantra` chs. 1–6 with Bhaṭṭa Rāmakaṇṭha, ed./tr. Goodall (1998) —
  `mātṛkotpatti`.
- `Parākhyatantra`, ed./tr. Goodall (2004) — nāda/bindu/śabdarāśi and the sphoṭa
  debate.
- Rāmakaṇṭha, `Nādakārikā` — Sferra 1991 (study), 2007 (levels of sound).
- `Parātrīśikā` + Abhinavagupta’s `Vivaraṇa`; Padoux 1990, 233–304; Bäumer 2011.
- Törzsök, “The Emergence of the Alphabet Goddess Mātṛkā in Early Śaiva Tantras”
  (2016) — the cleanest diachronic account of Mātṛkā.
- `Vāmakeśvarīmata`; Khanna 1986 (Śrīcakra/Śivānanda) for the E-triangle/kāmakalā
  material.
- Padoux, `Vāc` (1990) — the global synthesis the chapter says is still unmatched.
