tal-a28-v291 — So, just as the varied pleasures and pains of life do not destroy the liberated state of an awakened person, those same kinds of experiences at death do not destroy it either.

tal-a28-v292 — But those yogins who have become well established in a particular principle fix their mind on that very principle and, without doubt, go to it.

tal-a28-v293 — This is stated in the revered Svacchanda Tantra, beginning with the teaching about those who die through concentration on fragrance, and likewise the Malini scripture gives the fruits of the various concentrations.

tal-a28-v294 — For those who quickly abandon the body through concentrations, as if entering another body, there is no experience that can properly be called death.

tal-a28-v295 — The ordinary experience of death consists in a cutting shock at the vital points, delusion spreading through the senses, and a mental sinking into darkness. None of this is found in these yogins.

tal-a28-v296 — For the yogin maintains mental effort, establishing an I-sense in another body, as long as the movement of intellect continues there.

tal-a28-v297 — The circle of breath, dependent on that same process, moves along the path, and by that very means the circle of senses in the other body becomes awakened.

tal-a28-v298 — Just as bees rise when the bee-king rises and settle when he settles, so all the activities of the senses follow the mind.

tal-a28-v299 — Therefore, when he moves into another body, there is no inward merging of the senses as in ordinary death. Instead, a subtle continuity arises again, like a body.

tal-a28-v300 — Thus, for yogins who move into other bodies and likewise for those who move within bodies formed of various principles, there is no delusion.
