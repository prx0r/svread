tal-a28-v301 — These yogins should be known as twofold: worldly and initiated. The former become Shiva gradually, while the latter do so merely through experiencing that state.

tal-a28-v302 — Initiation, being divided by many higher and lower distinctions, yields varied fruits for yogins.

tal-a28-v303 — But those who are knowers are of two kinds, according to whether their knowledge trembles or not. Among them, those whose knowledge trembles clearly become Shiva at the end of the body.

tal-a28-v304 — Because knowledge has arisen in them, but it is not very clear due to association with other conceptual thoughts, nor is it uprooted in its nature.

tal-a28-v305 — Therefore, the conceptual thought that arises in the body from carelessness necessarily perishes when the body falls, and that supreme knowledge is then recognized.

tal-a28-v306 — That knowledge, not destroyed within but standing as latent conceptual traces, having reached ripeness, indeed shines forth when the obstruction is removed.

tal-a28-v307 — But those whose nature is well-practiced knowledge, who are always of the nature of Shiva, are indeed liberated while living; for them there is no question at all at the time of death.

tal-a28-v308 — For just as there is no question about the living-liberated being happy, unhappy, or deluded while they live, so too there is no such question at death.

tal-a28-v309 — The Supreme Lord said this in the holy Ratnamala scripture, and so did the Lord Ahi, bearer of the burden of supporting the universe, in his own scripture.

tal-a28-v310 — Whether in a street, amid urine and feces, in an outcaste's house, in hell, or in a cremation ground, whether with thought or without thought, the knower obtains liberation at the end.
