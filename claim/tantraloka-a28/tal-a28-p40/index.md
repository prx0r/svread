## Reading (machine draft, verse-mapped)

**tal-a28-v391** — Ucchuṣma, Śavara, Caṇḍa, Gumataṅga, Ghora, Antaka, Ugra, Hala, Halaka, Krodhin, and Huluhulu—these are the ten ancient teachers, all consisting of Śiva.
**tal-a28-v392** — By the sequence of their own partial modes of consciousness, they assume human bodies. Though their awareness and activity differ from one another, they are all full of knowledge.
**tal-a28-v393** — All of them are devoted to the service of meat, wine, union, initiation, worship, and scripture. Their inner difference lies in qualities such as pride, calm, anger, and forbearance.
**tal-a28-v394** — Having understood this, the wise student should always request a teacher who knows the complete scripture to give an explanation, doing so with worship beforehand.
**tal-a28-v395** — The teacher should give an explanation of such a scripture to his own student, or even to another's student, if the person is fit to hear it—even to a Vaiṣṇava or someone of a lower affiliation.
**tal-a28-v396** — A teacher filled with compassion may explain even to a low person, while avoiding the vital secret matters, because the descent of power can occur in many different ways.
**tal-a28-v397** — On a smeared, square seat on the ground, one should make three lotuses. On the lotus, one should make the seat of knowledge, measuring six and three finger-widths.
**tal-a28-v398** — In the middle one should worship Vāgīśānī; on the south and north, the teachers and Gaṇeśa; and on the lower lotus, the lord of the ritual tradition, using offering water, flowers, and libations.
**tal-a28-v399** — Using the offering vessel as in the general rite, the wise teacher should properly satisfy the circle and then give the explanation, beginning with the proper connection.
**tal-a28-v400** — By connecting aphorism, word, sentence, chapter, and the whole text in order, and by supporting an unbroken link between what comes before and after, one should lead the sentences forward.
