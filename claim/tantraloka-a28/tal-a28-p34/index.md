## Reading (machine draft, verse-mapped)

**tal-a28-v331** — Because of unseen merit, repeated practice, intensity, descent of power, and similar causes, one attains that very form, whether happy, suffering, or deluded.
**tal-a28-v332** — Or else, if one attains a state free from pleasure and pain, or one of bliss, why is that? Because that is what one remembers in consciousness.
**tal-a28-v333** — Whatever shone forth most strongly before becomes decisive, because this body is established in consciousness; what consciousness previously took as its basis through identification becomes that basis.
**tal-a28-v334** — This is what is called absorption in sattva, rajas, tamas, or in knowable things like blue and yellow: consciousness takes up a form that was previously constructed.
**tal-a28-v335** — Consciousness, by taking its stand, fully makes something into a basis; therefore, even if a body is something merely established, it differs from an inert thing like a wall.
**tal-a28-v336** — The body is distinct because it comes first among all things that consciousness establishes. Other things may also be identified with, but that is not known in the same foundational way.
**tal-a28-v337** — Because knowables depend on the body, and the body is constant and non-deviating, prior death and birth belong to that same body.
**tal-a28-v338** — By memory, whose impressions come from past experience, Kallata our lineage teacher reasons thus.
**tal-a28-v339** — In bodily indistinction, breath-firmness is stated as the cause; otherwise, what is the cause of breath-firmness?
**tal-a28-v340** — The question does not subside in bodily indistinction; remembering, one attains that form in the cause.
