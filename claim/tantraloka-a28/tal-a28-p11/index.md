**tal-a28-v101** — This is the mūrti-yāga, the foremost of all worships; but in a rite performed for a specific desire, it should be done seven times in the same way.
**tal-a28-v102** — First the Mothers recognize the house, then they perceive its capability and its strength or weakness, and afterward the Mothers are filled with amazement.
**tal-a28-v103** — Then they draw near, are pleased, and grant boons; and the goddesses and the Lord, together with their retinues, are fully present.
**tal-a28-v104** — Therefore this beloved mūrti-yāga should be performed by the wise in a pleasing, hidden house, with the heroes and the śaktis fully joined together.
**tal-a28-v105** — Participants may be brought in without prior arrangement through the recitation of deity names; but if the mūrti circle cannot be obtained, one should worship only maidens.
**tal-a28-v106** — For a desire-rite, one must not choose a maiden who is bodily impaired or menstruating; the rite is also tied to the first lunar day with Śravaṇa and the fourth day with the three Uttara stars.
**tal-a28-v107** — The fifth day is with Hasta; the sixth with the Pūrva stars and Punarvasu; the seventh and the next with Maghā; and likewise the ninth with Rohiṇī.
**tal-a28-v108** — The twelfth day is with Mūla, the fourteenth with Aśvinī, the full moon is included, and the new moon is with Dhaniṣṭhā; this forms an elevenfold set.
**tal-a28-v109** — A day joined with Sunday, Monday, Tuesday, or Friday, or another suitable day, is well known as a yoga-parvan, whether it occurs by night or by day.
**tal-a28-v110** — The mūrti-yāga should certainly be performed on a yoga-parvan; the wise person should honor all days called yoga-parvans with the mūrti-yāga.
