## Reading (machine draft, verse-mapped)

**tal-a28-v321** — It is a state where disgust and its opposite are broken, steady on all sides like a pillar, free from all calamity, and beyond valid cognition and mental constructs.
**tal-a28-v322** — Absorbed in that inner awareness, one may still act yet remain unstained. The key example is space in a pot: even when the pot is broken, space is not destroyed.
**tal-a28-v323** — Just as space does not perish when the pot is broken, so this self does not perish; the knower becomes Śiva-natured, independent, and extends into all things.
**tal-a28-v324** — For him there is neither mere being nor non-being, neither fixed form nor mental construction. This same point was spoken by the teacher in the Gītā.
**tal-a28-v325** — Whatever state one remembers while leaving the body at the end, that alone one attains, because one has always been shaped by dwelling on that state.
**tal-a28-v326** — Therefore remember me at all times and fight. When sattva is predominant, the one who dissolves then goes upward.
**tal-a28-v327** — If one dissolves in rajas and tamas, one goes deluded into a karmic birth; then there is confusion of the senses and distress marked by labored breathing.
**tal-a28-v328** — Experiences like these occur in the body and are not the same as abandoning the body; the real abandonment is only that single final moment consisting of the last breath.
**tal-a28-v329** — The instant immediately after which the body becomes like wood or a wall is the fractional moment of abandoning the body, the moment that separates one from it.
**tal-a28-v330** — At that very moment, free from the body's pleasure and pain, one remembers whatever is awakened by prior impressions.
