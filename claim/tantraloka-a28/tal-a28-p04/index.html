tal-a28-v31 — The fulfilment time is defined in six ways, by general and special forms. Among these, the first and fifth days of the month are called auspicious days.

tal-a28-v32 — Because of its excellence, a fulfilment day is spoken of with the prefix 'śrī'. Since this appointed time is secret, it should not be named without that proper prefix.

tal-a28-v33 — In both lunar fortnights, the fourth, eighth, and final days are declared the most general fulfilment times.

tal-a28-v34 — If, on those same general fulfilment days, a future conjunction involving a planet and lunar mansion occurs, that twofold factor makes the day a general-special case.

tal-a28-v35 — Shambhu taught that this classification becomes elevenfold in each case, and among cases of the same kind the excellent one is to be distinguished.

tal-a28-v36 — This verse gives a coded list of fortnight and planetary combinations used in the fulfilment-time scheme, pairing lunar markers with sequences of planets and luminaries.

tal-a28-v37 — This verse lists the lunar mansions relevant to the schedule, including Phalguni, Tishya, Shravana, Shatabhisha, Mula, Prajapati, and Vishakha.

tal-a28-v38 — This verse sets out coded calendrical markers together with specific times such as first night, midnight, daybreak, and midday for the ritual schedule.

tal-a28-v39 — At the appointed time called first night, in the months beginning with Margashirsha, the female messenger may be a maiden, a woman of low birth, or a courtesan, provided she is passionate and knows reality.

tal-a28-v40 — In expanded or condensed form and in proper order, worship should be done in the circle called the after-sacrifice. On fulfilment days this after-sacrifice circle should be performed with special emphasis.
