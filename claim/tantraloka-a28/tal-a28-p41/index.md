**tal-a28-v401** — Using principles such as the frog-leap and the lion-glance as appropriate, one should present the meaning of the scripture clearly, without mixing it up and without breaking the link between what comes before and after.

**tal-a28-v404** — When the subject is taught by first setting out a firmly formed prior view and then resolving it, it quickly enters the student's mind, free from doubt and misunderstanding.
**tal-a28-v405** — Whatever language, reasoning, style of discussion, level of absorption, or order becomes a means of awakening for the student, the teacher should adopt that form of explanation.
**tal-a28-v406** — After completing the subject being explained, there should be satisfaction and worship in the circle. Another subject may be taught after a chapter, but there should be no idle talk.
**tal-a28-v407** — At the end of the explanation, one should ask forgiveness, dismiss everything, and cast the remains into deep water. Worship should be performed especially at the beginning, middle, and end of the scripture.
**tal-a28-v408** — At the completion, one should perform special worship for the initiates. Even so, for one whose mind is undivided, there may be no need for expiations.
**tal-a28-v409** — Even so, for the grace of those who do not know the truth, one should act as taught in the revered Picu scripture. But if remembrance alone destroys sin, O Lord, how can an expiation rite still be prescribed?
**tal-a28-v410** — When the Goddess asked why an expiation rite is declared, the answer is: it is true that remembrance alone, even uttered once, can liberate one here.
