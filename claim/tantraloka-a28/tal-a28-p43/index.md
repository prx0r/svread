## Reading (machine draft, verse-mapped)

**tal-a28-v421** — One who maims Shakti or kills a yogin or knower, or who disrupts the occasional rites, should recite in a doubling sequence beginning with a hundred thousand.
**tal-a28-v422** — If someone under a vow, eating moderately and observing celibacy, goes elsewhere to take a messenger woman, deluded by desire, that is an offense.
**tal-a28-v423** — Then one should perform a recitation of a hundred thousand, as stated in the Brahmayamala; and at the end of initiation, consecration, and occasional rites, worship of the guru is required.
**tal-a28-v424** — According to the teaching of the Siddhayogishvari, it should always be done on the next day: worship of a guru endowed with the previously stated marks, a seer who knows the true principle of the Trika.
**tal-a28-v425** — That guru should always be accepted, leaving aside any other, even one present in the circle. One should make a svastika there and place upon it a golden or similar seat.
**tal-a28-v426** — Having prepared the seat, one should worship the path present there in its full extent; then, with devotion, one should request the guru to preside there.
**tal-a28-v427** — There he should be worshiped with his own mantras, in due order, with flowers, incense, arghya, respectful touching, fine garments, food offerings, and libations.
**tal-a28-v428** — After worshiping him fully, even to the directions, the disciple should honor him with gifts, offering all he has and, with devout awareness, even himself.
**tal-a28-v429** — By fully satisfying the guru with gifts, even one who knows the truth is freed from the bond of debt and thereby attains proper fitness for authority.
**tal-a28-v430** — According to the Skandayamala, one who does not perform guru worship is born for a hundred lifetimes and only then becomes fit for authority and attains liberation.
