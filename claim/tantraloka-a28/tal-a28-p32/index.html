tal-a28-v311 — The words 'even' and 'also' indicate his state of living liberation; the mention of being with thought or without thought refers only to a possible circumstance at that time.

tal-a28-v312 — Whether at a holy place or in an outcaste's house, even if memory is lost, one who abandons the body, being liberated simultaneously with knowledge, attains absolute freedom, with sorrow destroyed.

tal-a28-v313 — The Ananta-verses say that one might suspect good or bad deeds to bind him, but in fact they do not bind him.

tal-a28-v314 — From the word 'even' one might think death with memory intact is possible, but death usually involves lost memory; and if memory was lost before death, what does that matter?

tal-a28-v315 — The optative indicates mere possibility, and the time-expression shows that death is not the cause of liberation.

tal-a28-v316 — Although 'isolation' may raise doubt because a body remains, that body no longer produces difference, so sorrowlessness is established.

tal-a28-v317 — Just as connection with other bodies does not differentiate him, so too connection with his own body does not differentiate the living-liberated person.

tal-a28-v318 — Therefore, for one who is non-different from Śiva and has set aside all universal forms, there is no real difference whether he is in a body or not.

tal-a28-v319 — Even so, because suspicion based on old impressions of difference may persist, the text now speaks of aloneness; or else it does so from another standpoint.

tal-a28-v320 — Those things do not know him as different, and he is not known as different by them; such a one is called liberated. The revered Traiśirasa also speaks of a state beyond the pair of sun and moon.
