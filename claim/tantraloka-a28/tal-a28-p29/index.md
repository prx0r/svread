## Reading (machine draft, verse-mapped)

**tal-a28-v281** — The loss of one's bond of faith in knowledge, sacred abodes, and initiation is to be recognized by disturbed activities, and those activities are of two kinds.
**tal-a28-v282** — Some disturbances never arise when a person is healthy and appear only in illness, caused by bodily imbalance. These amount only to the experience of that condition.
**tal-a28-v283** — Bodily imbalance may awaken old worldly impressions, and they may slip in through temporary openings. But if they have mostly been burned by knowledge, they cannot grow again.
**tal-a28-v284** — But in those oriented toward isolation, some impressions never appear in good health and yet open up in illness. These arise from the descent of power.
**tal-a28-v285** — Ordinary worldly impressions are formed by long, deep habituation from the past. As the Lord of Serpents said in the sutra, they appear in those openings.
**tal-a28-v286** — In those oriented toward isolation, some cognitions arise that were never practiced before. Because of the nature of transmigration, these cannot come from habit and must come from the descent of power.
**tal-a28-v287** — These disturbed activities, when aggravated by bodily imbalance, lack settled certainty and reflective awareness, and they resemble the behavior of someone asleep or intoxicated.
**tal-a28-v288** — Even if disturbed behavior seems perverse and appears to defile knowledge, initiation, or the teacher, it should not be taken as true veiling when it has not taken root in the heart.
**tal-a28-v289** — For this very reason, even an awakened person, while still in the body, may undergo karma-born experiences at death, such as visions of Yama's servants, snakes, and the like.
**tal-a28-v290** — This alone does not mean he is not liberated, for death is an experience just like birth and ongoing life. Therefore death can be of two kinds, involving pain or pleasure.
