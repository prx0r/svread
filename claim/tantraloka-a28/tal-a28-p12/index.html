tal-a28-v111 — Through the mūrti-yāga, even without the mandala rite, a person can become a vow-holder; thus is the mūrti-yāga taught in the revered Siddhayogīśvarīmata.

tal-a28-v112 — Now the clear pavitraka rite taught by Śiva is explained, as it has been indicated in the revered Ratnamālā and Triśiras scriptures.

tal-a28-v113 — Among the teachings of the Siddha, Āṭana, Sadbhāva, Mālinī, and Sāra traditions, the procedure taught principally in the revered Mālā is now explained.

tal-a28-v114 — The serpent king, stupefied by a poison-born sleep from the churning of the milk ocean, could not remain in his own realm during the rainy season.

tal-a28-v115 — Pure and living only on air, he worshipped Lord Bhairava thoroughly for a hundred divine years, a span multiplied tenfold.

tal-a28-v116 — He appealed to the pleased Lord, saying that during the rains he was unable to remain in his own netherworld; and the Supreme Lord responded to him.

tal-a28-v117 — The Supreme Lord placed the serpent king upon the seat of his own matted locks; then all the hosts of gods bore him upon their heads.

tal-a28-v118 — For the great and honored, universal reverence is no wonder; therefore the serpent king came to rest on the head of the Great Lord and, in every way, on the gods as well.

tal-a28-v119 — One should prepare that pavitra for oneself, preceded by worship; even ten crores of acts of worship are not equal to the pavitra offering.

tal-a28-v120 — Without the pavitra and related rites, initiation is in vain, knowledge is in vain, and even worship of the guru is in vain; by Śiva's command, the serpent would take their fruit away.
