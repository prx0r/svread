tal-a17-v11 — The teacher said, "May he do thus," from the two nourishings of own form: the tāra syllable, the phoneme, and the address-word "you," hence beyond.

tal-a17-v12 — The action-word joined with the highest one is also a lamp from here; to you a name ending in the fourth case; then also a suitable lamp.

tal-a17-v13 — By the mantra-yoga of reasoning one should perform the respective action; indeed after invocation all action is declared.

tal-a17-v14 — Invocation and awakening are the establishment of one's own nature; there is absorption into one's own self-identity.

tal-a17-v15 — Shakti and earth are spoken of as having chief worshipfulness; yet, because of non-existence and non-difference, that worshipfulness is not of man and Shiva souls.

tal-a17-v16 — In inert principles, for consciousness to abide, the teacher first performs the invocation-division and then acts from the fourth division.

tal-a17-v17 — From the union that ends in salutation, one should posit full being; then, from the union or excess of that form, full-nature-ness.

tal-a17-v18 — The excess of the object of meditation arises from the power of the meditator's humility; having invoked and worshipped, one satisfies; this is explained in Sri Svacchanda.

tal-a17-v19 — Our teachers said: not by this path alone; but that which is worshipful by transcendence is of independent consciousness nature.

tal-a17-v20 — Because his light is unlimited, nothing can be manifested anywhere; yet for him, worshipfulness, meditatableness, and the like shine forth.
