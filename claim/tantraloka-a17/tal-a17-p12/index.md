## Reading (machine draft, verse-mapped)

**tal-a17-v111** — Thus, through the creation of pure principles, even the disciple becomes made of them; this is indicated in the Śrīmālinīvijayottara.
**tal-a17-v112** — The wise say that both bondage and liberation are the senses: restrained senses lead to bondage, while released senses lead to liberation.
**tal-a17-v113** — These, when in the pervasive state together with mind, may be released; but those, somewhere in an object, from obstruction, lead to bondage.
**tal-a17-v114** — Thus, in the Siddhayogishvara view, the state of the senses is declared to be twofold, distinguished as pure and impure.
**tal-a17-v115** — The glorious knowledge-teacher says to you: in the seeing of authority and praise, in initiation with all mantras, this rule is said.
**tal-a17-v116** — In purification ending in māyā, all actions would always be by the lower twofold-natured; but at the end of the manifest, in the unmanifest, by the supreme alone.
**tal-a17-v117** — Considering this rule—five limbs from pivana and the rest to īśa, ending with the whole—the supreme teacher should perform the action.
**tal-a17-v118** — On the former path, the countable number of offerings of truth and color should be doubled on the word path, becoming fourfold.
**tal-a17-v119** — Along the mantra-kalā path, by gradual steps the measure doubles and doubles until the three principles are purified, then it becomes twentyfold.
**tal-a17-v120** — For each rite, in the three-principle there would be sixty oblations; in the one-principle they say one hundred and eight oblations.
