tal-a17-v21 — How can enlightenment be in that form, when its independence causes difficult accomplishment?

tal-a17-v22 — In illumination, light would not be light without the power of object and the rest; the vocative ending alone is not enough.

tal-a17-v23 — To show what is taken from independence, there I perform this: 'I invoke the god'; then [I offer] a lamp to the god.

tal-a17-v24 — By prior utterance, the fullness-giving namaḥ, svāhā, and so forth may be praise; fullness increased by the conjunction of sun and moon is supreme.

tal-a17-v25 — One should ignite the nourishing, the sprinkling, and vauṣaṭ and the rest; there, even externally, action established in identity is urged.

tal-a17-v26 — If there is an action-word, the guru should sometimes infer it; it is employed for illuminating that unmanifested thing.

tal-a17-v27 — What is a mantra? It is that by which it becomes clear where it shines; by it, certainly, in sprinkling, pouring, japa, and similar rites.

tal-a17-v28 — Rather than accumulating that action, which makes one an obstructer, one should perform action-milking outside, where selfhood is absent.

tal-a17-v29 — Where a rite prescribes satisfaction, oblation, the enjoyer of oblations, burning of bonds, cutting, and similar acts, afterwards there are endless action-composed rites or acts.

tal-a17-v30 — What is to be accomplished by parts is not doubted there, as in initiation rites; then the disciple is tormented by a woman at the place of truth.
