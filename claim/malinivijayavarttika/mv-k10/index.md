**mv-k10-v1354** — First, for the sake of gaining the proper preparation, one uses decoction, emesis, and purgation; and the alteration brought about by initiation should not occur later during the ritual procedure.
**mv-k10-v1355** — Therefore the restriction of qualification arises from factors such as one's body and knowledge; for this reason Vaishnavas and others, who depend on the discipline of bound souls, are spoken of as limited in that way.
**mv-k10-v1356** — They are not qualified in the Shaiva scripture; Shaivas are not qualified in the left sphere; those are not qualified in the right; and those are not qualified in the Kula doctrine or in the Trika.
**mv-k10-v1357** — It is said in the revered Bhairava Kula that the teacher fit for the rite of the five initiations is one who has transcended the lower streams or who is grounded in the Trika scripture.
**mv-k10-v1358** — Thus there is a single qualification insofar as all rely on the self-principle, yet qualification becomes differentiated because of differences in preparation.
**mv-k10-v1359** — Therefore it is said in the Svacchanda Tantra that in every scripture Shiva is the fruit, because all arise from Shiva and all have Shiva's abode as their fruit.
**mv-k10-v1360** — And there again it is declared, in the discussion of the higher principles: what is not known by the Samkhyas, not by the great yogins, and not, O beloved, by the Pancharatrikas.
**mv-k10-v1361** — Thus, as many as three hundred and sixty-three disputants are challenged, for they discriminate only within that limited measure.
**mv-k10-v1362** — How could they be knowers whose very nature is knowledge of the unlimited Shiva principle? Now suppose they posit the presence of Shiva even in that mere partial portion.
**mv-k10-v1363** — If they were to superimpose that, it would not be clearly coherent; for in a sentence like 'Devadatta,' the reference is to a complete form in every respect.
**mv-k10-v1364** — How could someone who hears only the word 'Devadatta' superimpose upon that limited word the meaning of entire previous sentences?
**mv-k10-v1365** — And how could he understand so much meaning from the sentence? If one says, 'just from that,' then he is not merely a hearer of a word, nor—

**mv-k10-v1367** — If one were to construe Yoga, Samkhya, Arhata, Nyaya, Pancharatra, Veda, and Smriti as complete through the Shiva principle—
**mv-k10-v1368** — Let that be granted to that extent; but the full expansion is not there. It has been examined that knowledge of the Shiva principle must be preceded by the proper preparation.
**mv-k10-v1369** — Otherwise there would be a transgression for both teacher and student; but if the proper preparation is received, then the meaning of the scripture may indeed be of that kind.
**mv-k10-v1370** — But even after receiving higher and higher preparation, he does not cease to be Vaishnava, Samkhya, or Vedic in identity; he still continues to practice accordingly.
**mv-k10-v1371** — Then, if he remains Vaishnava and so on, his transgression is even greater. But surely, in this nondual state, what is this primary conceptual restriction?

**mv-k10-v1373** — When you say, 'Being a Vaishnava, why am I not qualified in the Shaiva domain?' you yourself are deluded by duality; and in duality, restriction applies by force.

**mv-k10-v1376** — Practices of contemplating identity with a deity, along with mantras, give their own results. Therefore, for someone whose self has been shaped by that contemplation and who desires a particular fruit, they bring success.

**mv-k10-v1378** — This indeed is the primary method: whoever is established in any domain and has permanently attained identity with it is authorized there.
**mv-k10-v1379** — For that very reason it is said that knowledge should be received from him, since he has attained identity through the ordered method of contemplating that.
**mv-k10-v1380** — But when a student is only then receiving the scripture in its proper sequence, how could he at first connect by his own understanding what is coherently taught in it?
