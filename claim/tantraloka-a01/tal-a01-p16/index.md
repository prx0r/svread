## Reading (machine draft, verse-mapped)

**tal-a01-v151** — Yoga is not different from action, and action is not different from yoga. The cognition that is firmly grounded in reality and brings the impressions of one's own mind to rest is what is called action.
**tal-a01-v152** — In one's own mind there are latent impressions born from karma, impurity, and maya. The cognition that arises to calm those impressions is essentially of the nature of consciousness itself.
**tal-a01-v153** — That same cognition, which rests upon the outer range of principles that give rise to the body, is action. And that very same cognition becomes yoga when it dissolves those principles back into consciousness.
**tal-a01-v154** — Even in everyday life, when one thinks 'I am going', there is first just an inner flash. That same flash enters the body, the place, and the senses, and then it becomes the act of going.
**tal-a01-v155** — Therefore, even what is called action is really nothing but knowledge. For that reason, it is right to say that knowledge alone brings liberation, and this has already been stated.
**tal-a01-v156** — Liberation is nothing other than the unfolding of one's own true nature. And the self's true nature is nothing other than consciousness. The verse then turns to what comes next.
**tal-a01-v157** — The powers of action and the rest are grounded in the nature of consciousness, because they cannot belong to anything unconscious, and no separate substrate for them can be established.
**tal-a01-v158** — In the scripture of the Supreme Lord, the powers are not said to have any separate substrate, as if they were qualities in the manner taught by the followers of Kaṇāda.
**tal-a01-v159** — If the powers of seeing, action, will, and so on were truly separate, then saying 'Shiva is one' would become mere words without any real basis.
**tal-a01-v160** — For this reason, freedom is nothing but consciousness itself; and when examined closely, it is seen to unfold into many powers.
