## Reading (machine draft, verse-mapped)

**tal-a01-v61** — Shiva is not fixed or limited. He is all-pervading and eternal, and he takes the form of the whole universe. Because he pervades everything, he is present everywhere; because he is eternal, he has no beginning or end.
**tal-a01-v62** — Because Shiva takes the universe as his form, he appears as the full variety of conscious and unconscious things. For this reason, texts such as the Dikshottara say that he has many forms.
**tal-a01-v63** — Shiva is said to appear in six ways: as the world, as a body, as light, as space, as sound, and as mantra. The mantric form also includes bindu, nada, and related expressions.
**tal-a01-v64** — Whoever becomes firmly established in a particular nature comes to share in that state. Since space, sound, and related forms are ways Shiva appears, true knowledge of them leads to supreme liberation without doubt.
**tal-a01-v65** — The Lord's having the universe as his form is only an indication of his nature. Even when he appears in limited forms, those limits dissolve within his unlimited being.
**tal-a01-v66** — The Kamika says that the Lord has all forms and yet is also formless. Like water or a mirror, he pervades everything, both moving and unmoving.
**tal-a01-v67** — Qualities such as pervasion and the rest are not really separate in Shiva. They are not divided from one another, but operate as one single quality that includes everything.
**tal-a01-v68** — So the straightforward conclusion is that Shiva is endowed with the power of freedom. Even when he is said to have many powers, those powers are not separate from that one power.
**tal-a01-v69** — Power is simply a being's own form as it is projected by the knowing subject. For that reason, Shiva remains nondual even when he is thought of as possessing power.
**tal-a01-v70** — When the Lord appears in different bodies as projected by the knowing subject, there is no real difference between them. It is like fire, which remains one whether it is burning or cooking.
