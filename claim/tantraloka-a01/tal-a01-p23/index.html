tal-a01-v221 — The limited self is defined by clear difference, and the Anava means based on that works through conceptual thought and determination; yet at its culmination it becomes nonconceptual.

tal-a01-v222 — An objection arises: how could intellect, mind, ego, and the individual self pervade Shiva, since they exist below him? How, then, can such a teaching be stated?

tal-a01-v223 — The answer is that, in reality, it is Shiva alone. Being self-luminous, he conceals his own nature and then appears again in that very form.

tal-a01-v224 — Even in dualistic scriptures such as the Matanga, this is clearly explained: the light that appears in the lower levels is the established manifestation of Shiva himself, who pervades downward.

tal-a01-v225 — For this reason, the supreme state shines even on the level of intellect and mind.

tal-a01-v226 — Both of these immersions indeed move toward the ocean of the nonconceptual, but without firm establishment in that state, nothing at all is accomplished.

tal-a01-v227 — One should not assume any difference in the fruit of awareness here; rather, it was indicated that conceptual construction has a primary role in this context.

tal-a01-v228 — Even in the view that makes validity depend on conceptual construction, the nonconceptual is still valid—but its validity is reached through the ordered sequence of those very concepts.

tal-a01-v229 — One who does not know the truth of the jewel may first practice through the means of certainty; by attaining the meansless, non-conceptual state, that person is called a knower of the jewel.

tal-a01-v230 — Here the means of non-difference is called śāmbhava, the means of both difference and non-difference is called śākta, and the means of difference is called āṇava.
