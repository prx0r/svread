## Reading (machine draft, verse-mapped)

**tal-a01-v31** — Anything apart from the free Self is insubstantial, and even such an insubstantial thing cannot be called liberation; it does not even receive a separate name.
**tal-a01-v32** — But the knowledge that manifests the true knowable reality in ever fuller measure, rising higher and higher, brings the quieting of each corresponding level of transmigration.
**tal-a01-v33** — The knowledge 'I am untainted by passion and the like, inwardly empty, and devoid of limited agency,' whether held briefly or in detail, liberates one to that extent.
**tal-a01-v34** — Thus, one who is liberated in one respect but still stands within another limitation is not truly liberated; only one free from all limitations is truly liberated.
**tal-a01-v35** — But the knowledge of the true knowable reality that is completely free from all limitations leaves no room anywhere for ignorance and gives true liberation.
**tal-a01-v36** — The nature of knowledge and ignorance described above is, in the Shaiva teaching, further divided in each case into two kinds: the personal and the cognitive.
**tal-a01-v37** — In that division, the person's ignorance is called impurity; arising from it and dissolving into it, its nature is to veil one's own full Shiva-state of perfect consciousness and action.
**tal-a01-v38** — That ignorance of the bound soul has the form of contracted knowing and doing, yet it is non-conceptual, because determinate intellectual operations such as judgment have not yet arisen.
**tal-a01-v39** — When, through reflection arising from the limited self within the cavity of the six sheaths, there arises a determining cognition of the form 'I know this in this way'—
**tal-a01-v40** — When such intellect arises, that kind of knowledge is called cognitive ignorance; in relation to it, the personal ignorance is both nourished and nourishing.
