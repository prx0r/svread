tal-a01-v241 — Thus, in the knowledge that operates through the means of the three powers, the last is the root, above it is the middle, and above that is the highest, first level.

tal-a01-v242 — Even beyond that is a supreme knowledge, free of means and the rest, resting in the power of bliss; here it is called the unsurpassed.

tal-a01-v243 — That self-luminous knowledge, whose true presence is rare even for the lords of knowledge and others, is spoken of in the revered Siddha Tantra.

tal-a01-v244 — This is also clearly indicated in the Mālinī, in the eighteenth chapter, by the statement that it is not attained without the grace of Śaṅkara.

tal-a01-v245 — By this very teaching in the Mālinīvijayottara, the fourfold knowledge that brings the great rise of accomplishment and liberation is systematized by me in this teaching called the Tantrāloka.

tal-a01-v246 — Here, whatever is reflected on, whether inwardly or outwardly, first shines forth with its form not yet unfolded.

tal-a01-v247 — That first manifestation appears with an unopened, indescribable form; doubt arises only when some form is determined, and not otherwise.

tal-a01-v248 — In the question 'what is this?', one part of the primary appearance is already well determined, while doubt remains because properties such as 'it is' or 'it is not' are not yet unfolded.

tal-a01-v249 — The word 'what' does not reveal any additional meaning; it simply denotes the thing itself while its form remains unopened.

tal-a01-v250 — The doubt 'is it a post or a person?' is not the primary kind of doubt; it is really the arising of determination among many possible properties.
