## Reading (machine draft, verse-mapped)

**tal-a01-v161** — Since liberation is the self's own shining forth, there is no reason to think that merely knowing a cause and its effect will automatically bring liberation.
**tal-a01-v162** — Although knowledge is sometimes described as the cause of liberation, that is not the primary way to understand their relation; so the objection raised earlier does not hold.
**tal-a01-v163** — Action is by nature knowledge itself, but as it takes on a grosser form in the self, its variety becomes visible.
**tal-a01-v164** — Within the path of action, the means are divided into inner and outer and then into many further kinds, so they become countless.
**tal-a01-v165** — This also removes the doubt some wise people had, that because the means differ, liberation itself might differ.
**tal-a01-v166** — Even if liberation comes about through different causes, such as the destruction of impurity and its power or the falling away of concealment, liberation itself is not different, just as the breaking of a pot is one kind of event though caused in different ways.
**tal-a01-v167** — This threefold division was taught by the Supreme Lord in the scripture called Śrīpūrva, in the section that determines the nature of penetration.
**tal-a01-v168** — When a person who is thinking of nothing at all is awakened by the teacher and penetration arises, that is called the śāmbhava penetration.
**tal-a01-v169** — When one contemplates a reality without utterance, using the mind alone, and attains penetration in that way, it is called the śākta penetration.
**tal-a01-v170** — The penetration that arises through utterance, bodily practice, meditation, letters, and imagined locations is properly called the āṇava penetration.
