## Reading (machine draft, verse-mapped)

**tal-a01-v171** — The expression 'one who thinks of nothing' means that conceptual construction is not in use, and through that there is a sudden absorption into what is to be known.
**tal-a01-v172** — How does that happen? It happens through a most weighty teacher, whose awareness is turned toward the knowable and whose awakening is immediately mature.
**tal-a01-v173** — The verse gives a grammatical explanation of the earlier expression and adds that, for the dependent self, penetration means sinking into its own true form.
**tal-a01-v174** — The meaning here is that one comes to share the nature of Shambhu, the primal Lord who is never separate from his power; this meaning is to be understood as shining forth on its own.
**tal-a01-v175** — Even without any determinate judgment, it suddenly appears reflected in the mirror of the subject, overwhelms the subject, and displays its own majesty.
**tal-a01-v176** — Depending on the degree of purity of the heart, the knowable appears in two ways: as mere consciousness, or as something inert and constructed.
**tal-a01-v177** — The other kind of knowable is also treated as real in its own way; penetration into what is inert takes the form of a reflected image.
**tal-a01-v178** — Penetration into consciousness is nothing other than identity with it; for that reason the awareness involved is non-conceptual and does not depend on contemplation or similar practices.
**tal-a01-v179** — Here śāmbhava penetration is the attainment of identity with Shiva; any determinate understandings that arise afterward come by its grace, not as its cause.
**tal-a01-v180** — Even if later determinate understandings arise in harmony with that identity, they cannot be its means, because they depend on conceptual construction, while the valid cognition in question is non-conceptual.
