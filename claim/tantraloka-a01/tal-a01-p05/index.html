tal-a01-v41 — But when the bound soul's conditioning has diminished and the person has reached a higher state, there arises an expansive, non-conceptual personal liberating insight.

tal-a01-v42 — To the extent that expansive non-conceptual knowledge is present, the corresponding cognitive knowledge remains, as before, both nourished by and nourishing the personal knowledge.

tal-a01-v43 — Although personal ignorance may be destroyed by initiation and the like, that knowledge becomes clearly manifest at the end of that body.

tal-a01-v44 — But when the expansion of cognitive ignorance dissolves through cognitive knowledge, liberation while living stands right in the palm of one's hand.

tal-a01-v45 — Initiation too is truly liberating when it is preceded by cognitive liberating insight; therefore, even there, cognitive knowledge has primacy.

tal-a01-v46 — This twofold division within knowledge and ignorance has been taught in the Svayambhuva, the Ruru, the Matanga and others, and by revered teachers such as Khetapala.

tal-a01-v47 — For attaining that cognitive liberating insight which takes the form of such determinate understanding, scripture alone is primary, because it reveals the knowable reality.

tal-a01-v48 — Even when inner personal ignorance has fallen away through initiation, conceptualization may still arise, because what resides in the intellect has not yet ceased.

tal-a01-v49 — Because the mind takes the self to last only as long as the body exists, there would be no liberation even at the end of the body, since human ignorance would still not have been removed.

tal-a01-v50 — But when Buddhist ignorance ceases through the uprooting of conceptualization, liberation occurs right then; this is what the author stated in the revered Niśāṭana.
