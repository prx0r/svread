tal-a01-v201 — For this reason the lord of Śrīvidyā respectfully said: if conceptual construction did not make your nonconceptual nature an object for sense-born cognition, then clear experiences with varied images inscribed within would not arise.

tal-a01-v202 — This is stated in the Śrīmataṅga and other texts, where the abode of the Lord is described, with various qualifications, as consisting of the rays of his own power.

tal-a01-v203 — In that divine light there is a brilliantly radiant, motionless presence with an unmoving form; that alone is the supreme, subtle culmination, whose nature is nectar in all directions.

tal-a01-v204 — With all coverings destroyed, peaceful, intent on reality alone, free from beginning and end, and pure, that state is nevertheless spoken of figuratively as having a form.

tal-a01-v205 — This figurative way of speaking has a clear reason and purpose: through it clarity quickly arises in the qualified subject, and that leads to abiding in full absorption in that reality.

tal-a01-v206 — Those very qualities, called powers, are worshipped in various fitting forms so that one may become fully absorbed in that reality.

tal-a01-v207 — In that context, an unlimited power or even limited powers may be drawn in, according to the principle by which what is invoked becomes present, and according to nearness or distance.

tal-a01-v208 — Because of its pervasiveness, that reality draws in all powers, along with fullness, luminosity, the nature of consciousness, and the state of being Bhairava.

tal-a01-v209 — Sadashiva and the other principles below the highest, lacking full upward pervasion, can draw in powers only to the extent that their worship is near or far.

tal-a01-v210 — In this condition, the path taught here is called the Shakta path: it is a conceptual course, because it works through distinctions and their counterparts.
