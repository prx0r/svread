## Reading (machine draft, verse-mapped)

**tal-a01-v211** — One who is established in the nonconceptual path, by whatever route they enter—from the earth principle up to Sadashiva—through that very route becomes Shiva.
**tal-a01-v212** — In a pure heart, illumined by the first flash of that level, awareness comes to recognize itself as supreme Shiva through that very opening.
**tal-a01-v213** — Thus, those who live close to the wise teacher know this good means, which partakes of supreme will-power, as the Shambhava immersion.
**tal-a01-v214** — Now the Shakta mode is described: it works clearly through mind, intellect, thought, and ego-making, in a conceptual way, dealing with things like will that are, in reality, made of maya.
**tal-a01-v215** — The Shakta mode works through identification and through the sequence of intention and determination; even though it is a means based in maya, at its end it becomes nonconceptual.
**tal-a01-v216** — The nonconceptual state that appears in a bound soul is indeed called Shambhava, but it is incomplete because of the defect of the limited knower; when that defect falls away, it blossoms fully.
**tal-a01-v217** — Thus, in the Shakta mode there is a conceptual level marked by the awareness of agency; even when that awareness is clear, it still involves contraction, as explained earlier.
**tal-a01-v218** — Likewise, for one who is intent on dissolving the whole mass of contraction, that power becomes radiant and can bring about the inner appearance of whatever is desired.
**tal-a01-v219** — One might ask whether conceptual cognition exists in the Anava means. The answer is that, as defined here, the Anava means is marked by the absence of utterance, so that kind of conceptual operation belongs to another means.
**tal-a01-v220** — Here the word utterance covers outward expression and related operations. These are not what define the Shakta means, because the Shakta means is characterized by the play of difference and nondifference, which is the very nature of power.
