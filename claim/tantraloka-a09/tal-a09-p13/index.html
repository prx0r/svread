tal-a09-v121 — Action, of the nature of merit and demerit and marked by pleasure, pain, and the rest, is to be recognized by its own effect, from its being a cause.

tal-a09-v122 — A cause never exists at any time without its own effect; this fitness is causality itself, conditioned by the immediacy of the fruit.

tal-a09-v123 — But the causality of the earlier one, and what is that, is observed through pleasure, pain, and the rest, where a cause is seen in common.

tal-a09-v124 — By various other causes, something besides: here action is seen, born in one's own limb as grace, roughness, and the like, from one's own action.

tal-a09-v125 — Seen thus, action is posited as the cause dwelling in another body; here too, in other bodies, the fruit of action clearly appears, because.

tal-a09-v126 — Farming done in spring is enjoyed in autumn, and another body comes of it; as for one investigator, the origin is from here and there.

tal-a09-v127 — Its fruit is manifold, the action that is ancient; even a drunk king, if he were to command a cook in the morning just so.

tal-a09-v128 — Without the intention 'feed him' he obtains that fruit; thus even karma won in another birth ripens by present intention.

tal-a09-v129 — Without doing it again he enjoys the fruit, the cause there being the unshakability of the earlier [intention]; therefore action done by deed or by austerity too.

tal-a09-v130 — Or it would be restrained by knowledge, though bent on the ripening of the fruit; but the action begun in this body, that karma, how then?
