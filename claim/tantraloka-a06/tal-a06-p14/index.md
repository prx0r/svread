**tal-a06-v131** — The year of the Fathers is a day and night, from the north-south course; by their own measure that year is called divine.
**tal-a06-v132** — Three hundred sixty years here are human; that, divided by twelve, yields the month-number here.
**tal-a06-v133** — Having destroyed that day-night reckoning by thirty, one should say it; having destroyed it again by twenty-one thousand and six hundred, one should say it.
**tal-a06-v134** — There one should state the breath-count, with its sixty-year beginning; again it was said by teachers in their own commentaries beginning with the glorious Raurava.
**tal-a06-v135** — The day-night of the gods is a year for humans; by three hundred and sixty, the year of men becomes a god-year.
**tal-a06-v136** — And in the glorious Svachanda scripture that very opinion is seen: beginning with that day-night of the ancestors, and afterwards.
**tal-a06-v137** — Thus the divine day-night is indeed concluded in unity; by that, the teachers who derive it from the two statements of the glorious Svachanda.
**tal-a06-v138** — They deludedly called the ancestral year a divine day, in vain; thousands of divine sun-years occur in the ages, from four onward.
**tal-a06-v139** — By one each decrease, among those hundreds there are eight junctions; fourteen Manus span seventy-one four-ages.
**tal-a06-v140** — There in Brahma's hand fourteen Indras proceed in order; at the end of Brahma's day, the flame of time-fire measures a lakh yojanas.
