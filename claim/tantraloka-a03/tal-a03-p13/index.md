## Reading (machine draft, verse-mapped)

**tal-a03-v120** — Clearly manifest mere light is called the sun. That aspect which pours forth the essential content of what is to be illuminated is called the moon.
**tal-a03-v121** — They call the sun the means of knowing and the moon the object to be known. Though never separated from each other, the two also stand as independent.
**tal-a03-v122** — When this pair, as enjoyer and enjoyed, turns toward each other, there arises a blazing form of consciousness that is proclaimed as fire.
**tal-a03-v123** — The supreme principle of fire is nothing other than the knower's own reality. It is consciousness itself, not dependent on anything else because it is identical with what is to be known.
**tal-a03-v124** — Because of its freedom, consciousness is called the knower. It appears varied through differences among knowables, expanded by the diverse appearances of objects that arise on the moon side and are taken up by the fire aspect.
**tal-a03-v125** — For this reason the great Lord called this fire Citrabhanu. The status of consciousness is entirely independent of the whole set of means that begin with the knowable.
**tal-a03-v126** — Consciousness stands as the immediate sense, 'I am the knower.' But one who knows like a person who merely knows scripture is not truly knowing in essence, because that knowledge depends on experienced objects rather than on its own self-form.
**tal-a03-v127** — That is not true knowership which depends on a pure knowable or anything similar. In such a state, the term knowership is used only in a secondary sense, because of a certain fitness.
**tal-a03-v128** — That dependent knowing is only a kind of measuring, constructed by the limited knower. Consciousness, even when it surges forth, is not divided by temporal sequence.
**tal-a03-v129** — As soon as consciousness arises, it is already full and contains within itself the knower, the known, and the rest. Ordinary actions like cooking, by contrast, unfold in sequence because they are limited by time.
