tal-a03-v50 — Even if some cause is designated as an external original image, it too becomes a reflection in consciousness; otherwise, outside of consciousness, it would be unreal.

tal-a03-v51 — Thus, protected by the firm weapon of the logic of one's own awareness, the sovereignty of reflection alone expands everywhere.

tal-a03-v52 — An objector asks how a reflection can exist without an original image. The reply is: what can we do, for it is seen? The objector is then challenged to identify that supposed original image.

tal-a03-v53 — It is not so, because the supposed original lacks the required definition. What is truly called an original image is something unmixed with others, independent, and truly appearing, just like a face.

tal-a03-v54 — They define the nature of a reflection as resembling another form without abandoning its own nature, like reflections seen on a sword, a mirror, or a flat surface.

tal-a03-v55 — And it is said by the Buddhists that even if external objects exist, cognition is one because it perceives many; it takes on forms resembling many, but it does not itself become many.

tal-a03-v56 — But what, then, is the definition of a reflection? It is stated as the impossible manifestation of difference from its substrate due to the unfitness for mixing with another, just as a face appears in a mirror.

tal-a03-v57 — This universe, beginning with the supreme reality, is mixed with consciousness and cannot be manifested as different from consciousness; why, then, should it not be called a reflection in consciousness?

tal-a03-v58 — If this arrangement of the definition is arbitrary, then let the universe be called the original image. Wise people are concerned with reality itself, not with conventional terminology.

tal-a03-v59 — But surely a reflection cannot exist without an original image? What of that? In a reflection, the original image does not exist as a separate entity, for it exists only in identity with it.
