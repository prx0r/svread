## Reading (machine draft, verse-mapped)

**tal-a03-v100** — If the great Lord, master of the dissolution and arising of endless varieties of power, were to remain in only one form, then what would follow?
**tal-a03-v101** — He would abandon his great-lordship and his nature as consciousness, like a pot or any other finite thing. For limited luminosity is indeed the defining mark of the inert.
**tal-a03-v102** — Awareness is distinct from the inert because it cannot be measured. Therefore, his own powers surge up like waves in the great ocean of awareness.
**tal-a03-v103** — Like waves, they take on the variegation of their own self-union. That mutual diversity of the powers is the diversity of their own inner union.
**tal-a03-v104** — They declare this very diversity to be the supreme, clear body of the power of action, in that fourteenth abode where the three powers have become manifest.
**tal-a03-v105** — Therefore the Teacher declared this to be the trident in the Śrīpūrva teaching, and teachers who see reality have also called it stainless.
**tal-a03-v106** — The power-holder seems affected because will, knowledge, and action appear separately; yet power itself is never affected by anything.
**tal-a03-v107** — That same triad, through the power-holder's own modes such as willing and the rest, appears clearly; but when it shines forth in unity, then it does so as one.
**tal-a03-v108** — Because the three powers cannot be grasped by any one-sided condition and mutually resist such limitation, they become a single vibrant trident. The yogi who quickly becomes absorbed in that trident becomes stainless.
**tal-a03-v109** — In this way, the octad that begins from the supreme nectar state, when divided through Brahmi and the other forms, clearly expands into eight octads.
