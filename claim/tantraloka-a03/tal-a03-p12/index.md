## Reading (machine draft, verse-mapped)

**tal-a03-v110** — Here the supreme power, even while making her own body manifest, takes the form of bindu because of the stain associated with the knowable aspect.
**tal-a03-v111** — When the power of action has arisen in the abode of moon, sun, and fire, the undivided light that is present there is, for us, the supreme bindu.
**tal-a03-v112** — And in the teaching on the protection of the principles, the supreme Lord said that the bindu stands within the circle of the heart-lotus and has a nature consisting of the human, power, and Shiva.
**tal-a03-v113** — The bindu should be understood, through the distinction of dissolution, as pure and delivering. It is that sound whose nature is nada and which is present in all beings.
**tal-a03-v114** — Though described by divisions of below and above, it remains without activity. Whatever variety appears, such as soothing or sharp qualities and white or red colors, belongs to that apparent diversity.
**tal-a03-v115** — That light is in itself independent of those variations, as the teacher said. Neither the sun, nor the moon, nor even fire illuminates it.
**tal-a03-v116** — Sun, moon, and fire have no splendor of their own apart from that light. Rather, it is consciousness that shines in this way.
**tal-a03-v117** — Joined with varied conditions that arise from its own freedom, the one light takes on an inner variety, such as sharpness and other qualities.
**tal-a03-v118** — Even the sun, which is hard to look at, becomes delightful to the eyes when its light falls on water. See how powerful a conditioning medium can be.
**tal-a03-v119** — The light seen in the sun and other things is stained by limiting conditions, and therefore it depends on the Maheshvara light, the light of consciousness itself.
