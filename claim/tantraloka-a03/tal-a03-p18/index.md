## Reading (machine draft, verse-mapped)

**tal-a03-v170** — The true principle of desire is revealed as fullness in union, and the true principle of poison is revealed as nectar when concealment falls away.
**tal-a03-v171** — The pervading power is called poison because it covers the light of what does not pervade. The supreme abode is stainless in itself, but in relation to that power it appears stained.
**tal-a03-v172** — Because the universe, whose nature is action-power, flashes forth from that source, will is called desire, knowledge is called poison, and action is called the goddess, the stainless one.
**tal-a03-v173** — Absorption in this triad is called Śiva, Bhairava. Our teachers said that one should always become firmly established in that state.
**tal-a03-v174** — Having entered the poison-principle, one should shine forth with the contemplation 'I alone,' where there is no being, no poison, and no seizing force.
**tal-a03-v175** — An objection is raised: if origin is said to come from the ṣaṇṭha letters, how can ṣaṇṭha-ness apply here? The answer is that in this case the ṣaṇṭha is not the producer.
**tal-a03-v176** — For the will-power present there is the one that has been declared, and that very will-power produces its own task inwardly, in the form of what it desires.
**tal-a03-v177** — What stands there as the cause of tinging is the object to be willed. From that aspect there is no true production; what arises from it is turbidity and its form.
**tal-a03-v178** — They know the ṣaṇṭha as a collective joined with tinging from the knowable, because it does not function as a primary abode of either agitation or nonagitation.
**tal-a03-v179** — Because this group of four letters shines forth through its own inner heat, Bhairava of pure nature declared its name to be ūṣma.
