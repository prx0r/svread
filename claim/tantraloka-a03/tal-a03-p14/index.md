## Reading (machine draft, verse-mapped)

**tal-a03-v130** — Even if one thinks of an action as ending in a final moment, that alone does not make it the completed act of cooking. In this way, the principle of light is established as having the nature of moon, sun, and fire.
**tal-a03-v131** — Even so, the primary nature of these three is not removed: they remain mere light. Of these, the first, short form is called the sun.
**tal-a03-v132** — Because of long rest arising from stirring and bliss, it is called Soma. That supreme state, named Pluta, is established beyond the bliss of Soma.
**tal-a03-v133** — Those skilled in scripture call that light-natured state fiery. Here, when the three abodes are established, what remains is mere light.
**tal-a03-v134** — In scripture this is spoken of as the bindu and is regarded as the Shiva-bindu. It is indeed other than the letter ma, bearing only its shadow.
**tal-a03-v135** — Ra, la, and ha become established in the forms of ṣa, ṇ, ṭha, and visarga, just as the vowel i becomes another vowel through the shadow of a part of ra.
**tal-a03-v136** — In the same way, from the great Lord arises another principle. Even while appearing as a vowel in two ways, that inner desire to emit within him is declared to be the supreme Kauliki power.
**tal-a03-v137** — That very same power, through the force of stirring, certainly becomes visarga. This is said in the Trishiras scripture in the discussion that concludes with the pervasion of kala.
**tal-a03-v138** — The seventeenth kala, therefore having the form of nectar, spreads forth through the movement of the bindu of its own higher and lower nature.
**tal-a03-v139** — When devoid of visarga, that power which illuminates all things is called Shakti-Kundalini, and likewise Prana-Kundalini.
