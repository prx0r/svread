tal-a03-v140 — But at the terminal point of visarga it is called supreme Kundalini, and also the space of Shiva; it is called the supreme abode of the self of Brahman.

tal-a03-v141 — Mere visarga is the Lord's play of creation and dissolution: it is the casting of his own self into his own self, the state belonging to visarga.

tal-a03-v142 — Visarga, thus emitted and having reached a contracted state, is defined as Hamsa, as breath, as a consonant, and as a stop consonant.

tal-a03-v143 — That unsurpassed supreme abode is called Akula. The visarga of that Lord is called the Kauliki power.

tal-a03-v144 — That very same state of visarga, when bliss rises in sequence, becomes a surging forth that extends to clearly manifest action-power.

tal-a03-v145 — Visarga indeed, having drawn in so much otherness within itself, is like the ocean in this form: an endless succession of waves.

tal-a03-v146 — Therefore this visarga, consisting of the unmanifest ha and ka, is called the principle of Kama in the revered Kula cave.

tal-a03-v147 — That imperishable unmanifest, situated in the throat of the beloved, is of the nature of sound, without desire, and free from meditation and concentration.

tal-a03-v148 — Having fixed the mind there, one should subdue the world all at once. Therefore, just as visarga abides clearly in Hamsa, so too is its state understood.

tal-a03-v149 — In the same way, those beginning with Anuttara abide as the range from ka to sa. From Anuttara comes the clear emanation of the fivefold ka-group.
