tal-a03-v160 — When the supreme meets its own sameness, a twofold movement arises. One of these remains simply the supreme and can give rise to bliss.

tal-a03-v161 — In that joining, a clearly agitated union arises. Even here the supreme abode is present, and the second aspect also serves the purpose of bringing something forth.

tal-a03-v162 — Without agitation, the supreme nature is not sufficient. Will may lack action, or it may be tinged by what is to be willed.

tal-a03-v163 — Divided by swiftness and firmness, will becomes threefold, appearing as unopened, opening, and fully opened.

tal-a03-v164 — What is willed appears threefold within will without losing its essential form, brought forth by will's own inner heat, which is the impulse of its freedom.

tal-a03-v165 — When the triad of śa, ṣa, and sa is clearly projected outward, it stands forth; and from that, in the letter sa, the whole universe shines clearly.

tal-a03-v166 — Yogins declare that this is the immortal, supreme abode. In the cessations at the beginning and end of agitation, that very state is known as the supreme nectar.

tal-a03-v167 — In absorptions marked by the hissing sound, bliss, and the sense of true being, that very state is declared to be the supreme, undivided absolute.

tal-a03-v168 — The Blessed Lord himself taught this in the glorious Kula mystery, a text whose true name arises from the unity of power and the holder of power.

tal-a03-v169 — Out of affection, he revealed to you the poison-principle called anacka, shaped like a crow's beak-cavity and beyond meditation and concentration.
