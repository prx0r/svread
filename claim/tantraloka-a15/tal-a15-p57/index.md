## Reading (machine draft, verse-mapped)

**tal-a15-v561** — The names of the guru, the scripture, and the goddesses should not be uttered casually in mantra outside of worship; when properly invoked, they should then be satisfied.
**tal-a15-v562** — One should not perform tarpaṇa for a mantra that has merely arrived, because it takes away half of one's body; so the Blessed Lord declared.
**tal-a15-v563** — In the revered Ūrmi scripture it is taught that one must not publicize the conduct of goddesses and vīras, must not feel disgust toward it, and must not speak without the ritual substance in hand.
**tal-a15-v564** — One should speak the guru's name preceded by śrī, especially when holding an offering; one must not step over the shadow of the guru and other revered figures, and one must not associate with sectarian rivals.
**tal-a15-v565** — When talking, one may explain the meaning of one's own scripture, but should not speak otherwise or give hints. In an occasional rite, one should not add special worship beyond the daily worship.
**tal-a15-v566** — In the middle of the year, and especially during the pavitraka rite, one devoted to another mantra should not take part, and another scripture should not be followed.
**tal-a15-v567** — Knowledge that is not firmly rooted can be shaken by the views of others. Household implements and weapons should be honored through fire and offering.
**tal-a15-v568** — One should not touch or step over worshipful things with the feet. When the guru and his group come to the house, one should show them some special honor.
**tal-a15-v569** — One should not criticize initiated people out of hostility. If correction is given for instruction and without hostility, it is not a fault.
**tal-a15-v570** — One should not live closely with those who hold lower views, such as Vaishnavas and others. One should not eat or sleep with them, nor reveal one's own spiritual state to them.
