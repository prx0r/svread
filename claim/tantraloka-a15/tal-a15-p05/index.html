tal-a15-v41 — By firmly establishing it, the clear-minded practitioner attains the state of Shiva. Having become Shiva, he undertakes to do whatever he wishes.

tal-a15-v42 — An external means identified with Shiva is not by itself what gives liberation. Liberation comes when teacher and disciple firmly hold the recognition: this is Shiva; I am indeed Shiva.

tal-a15-v43 — For the two of them, firm identification, understood as cause and its bearer, is what liberates. The external does not succeed without the inner, and the inner does not succeed when cut off from the external.

tal-a15-v44 — Success comes through knowledge and action together, and this paired principle shines forth. He established this by citing the revered Brahmayamala, where it says, 'O God...'

tal-a15-v45 — And in the revered Ananda scripture it is said that the wise person has no impurity. Rather, bathing and wearing good clothes serve to produce contentment.

tal-a15-v46 — There, through the familiar sequence of purifying the body and related measures, those who bathe can enter inwardly without effort. From that, purity arises.

tal-a15-v47 — The bathing of the God of Gods is connected with what is called his eight forms. In that context, the true immersion is the inward plunging into the burning of impurity lit by mantra.

tal-a15-v48 — There, centered in the heart of the chosen mantra, one should go and return three steps within the space of cow dust and perform the earthly bath. That bath gives steadiness.

tal-a15-v49 — Having removed impurity with clay consecrated by the weapon mantra, one should then wash the body in order from head to feet with waters consecrated by the five-limb mantras.

tal-a15-v50 — One should immerse while reciting the root mantra together with its limbs, or through becoming one with it. After rising, one should contemplate in the sky the sun that contains all the luminous deities.
