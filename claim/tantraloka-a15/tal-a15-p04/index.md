## Reading (machine draft, verse-mapped)

**tal-a15-v31** — In the seedless initiation, one should purify the bond of pledge-conduct. Liberation can come through initiation alone, together with constant devotion to God and to the guru.
**tal-a15-v32** — This seedless initiation is said to give immediate liberation, because it cuts away the three bonds of past, future, and already-begun karma.
**tal-a15-v33** — If a purified person gives up the body at the end of initiation, he reaches the supreme state. But if the body is given up in the case of seeded initiation, the result fails because karma is absent.
**tal-a15-v34** — The initiated person must always guard the bond of pledge-conduct. The guru himself should first ask, understand, and carefully examine the candidate.
**tal-a15-v35** — When the guru wishes to give the right initiation, he should rely on the candidate's corresponding disposition. If the descent of grace has come to someone, the initiating guru, with regard to that initiation,
**tal-a15-v36** — must not show disrespect. This is established as the command of Shambhu. If the person is poor, the guru himself should perform the initiation using his own wealth.
**tal-a15-v37** — A disciple may even beg for initiation with nothing more than durva grass and water. Or the disciple may first give to the guru wealth that is his own or that he has obtained by begging.
**tal-a15-v38** — By giving it, that wealth becomes purified and fit to be offered. In this matter, the first requirement is the entry into the freedom of the state of Shiva.
**tal-a15-v39** — That person alone should be made the guru, for he is capable of giving initiation. His entry into the state of Shiva has been shown to come about through many means.
**tal-a15-v40** — One gradual path takes an external form, using bathing, placement, worship, and similar acts. Among these many practices, one establishes the state of Shiva in the heart.
