## Reading (machine draft, verse-mapped)

**tal-a15-v201** — That same illuminated field, gentle like the touch of moonlight, gives rise in this way to the first set of four directions through resting in the middle.
**tal-a15-v202** — With another set of four, the directions become eight, each presided over by its own power; all this arises within the supreme, boon-granting light of Śiva.
**tal-a15-v203** — The division of directions is established both in the world and in scripture, for Sadāśiva is taught in sequence as having a body made of five mantras.
**tal-a15-v204** — By the division into Īśāna, Aghora, Vāma, Sadyojāta, and the lower face, the directions are mapped out: Īśāna is above because of its luminosity, and the extending eastern face corresponds to that light.
**tal-a15-v205** — Puruṣa is identified in this scheme, the fierce Aghora is the south, the gentle Vāma is the left, and Sadyojāta, being turned away, is designated as the west.
**tal-a15-v206** — The nether face is below because of its lack of luminosity, and indeed space, wind, fire, water, and earth are the faces of that Lord.
**tal-a15-v207** — As the primary element, space alone is said to be above and made of light, while below that same principle is clearly non-luminous.
**tal-a15-v208** — In the middle, what is luminous is neither merely something to be illuminated nor simply its opposite; because it is seen through luminosity, a set of four directions arises there.
**tal-a15-v209** — Thus the Lord whose body is the five mantras is the sovereign of all directions, and after him Īśa, Rudra, Viṣṇu, and Brahmā are likewise established.
**tal-a15-v210** — Viṣṇu and Brahmā do not have a fifth face, because they are powers of differentiation concerned with maintenance and creation, not with that higher upward manifestation.
