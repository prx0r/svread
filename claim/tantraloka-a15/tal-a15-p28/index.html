tal-a15-v271 — The non-conceptual state is reached through streams of thought centered on identity with Shiva. Otherwise, how could that pure reality, which lives by the very life of reflective awareness, be approached?

tal-a15-v272 — How could any form of that whose nature is to shine ever remain unexamined by awareness? Therefore, by the power of its sovereign free will, this reality accomplishes even what seems most difficult.

tal-a15-v273 — Even though breath, intellect, and the like appear to shine, one should not conceive them as having their own independent nature. On the contrary, their nature is opposite to that of the supremely free self.

tal-a15-v274 — If one ascertains these faculties only as perishable and dependent on the Lord, one sinks into a certainty that cuts one off into participation in samsara.

tal-a15-v275 — Instead, one should take refuge in a non-conceptual abiding that comes through certainty in the eternal and so forth. But those who are endowed with purity born of the most intense descent of grace are different.

tal-a15-v276 — It has been explained that those purified by the most intense grace do not perform initiation and nyasa. But in this way, one who has the universe as his body clearly attains the state of being the universal self.

tal-a15-v277 — From nyasa alone, the body becomes transformed in that way, and one should worship it with flowers and other offerings. This may be done in detail with separate mantras, or briefly with the root mantra.

tal-a15-v278 — With incense, food offerings, acts of satisfaction, and so on, worship may be done in elaborate or concise form. Because samsara moves in a leftward way, all of this should be done with the left hand.

tal-a15-v279 — The teacher should also perform the rite of offering with that unimpaired hand. In the revered Matanga and other texts, the word 'left' is said to signify the secret.

tal-a15-v280 — The mantra practitioner devoted to left practice should perform the worship; this is stated clearly in the revered Bharga-shikha scripture and likewise in the revered Agama teaching.
