## Reading (machine draft, verse-mapped)

**tal-a15-v241** — On the back, extending through the three regions, one performs the installation of the three principles. Then comes the installation of the eight Ghoras, with the head formed by their feet.
**tal-a15-v242** — Then comes the installation of the being of Shiva, joined with one's own body. When this fivefold set has been performed, whatever holds primacy within it should become the main focus.
**tal-a15-v243** — In the sixth installation, one should place the object of contemplation and worship together with its limbs. Therefore, the Lord Ratiśekhara is the one fit to be installed here.
**tal-a15-v244** — The ninefold is to be installed above. When it is given primacy, the other is placed in a different abode. The same rule applies if the being of Bhairava is made primary.
**tal-a15-v245** — Ratiśekhara is to be installed beforehand in the place appropriate to what is being worshipped. This is how the tradition was set forth to me in the Śrīpūrva scripture.
**tal-a15-v246** — In the procedure of installation, the Lord Śambhu is the deity. How could he be excluded? His limbs also exist and are part of it.
**tal-a15-v247** — The form, emanation, and the three principles, together with the form and limbs, are to be understood as belonging to Shiva with his limbs. The installation is declared to be sixfold.
**tal-a15-v248** — Above this, one should then perform the sixfold Shakta installation. First comes Parāparā with her faces, and thus Mālinī at the beginning.
**tal-a15-v249** — After that, the triad beginning with Parā is installed in order at the crest, heart, and feet. Then the sequence continues through the head, face, throat, heart, navel, secret place, and thighs.
**tal-a15-v250** — The sequence beginning with Aghorā is placed in the knee and foot. Then come the five limbs of the vidyā. After that, one should invoke the Shakti whose form is the being of the Mother.
