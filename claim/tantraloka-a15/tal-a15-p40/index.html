tal-a15-v391 — In flowers and so on, and even in a child, the primary consecration is that grounded in recognition of Śiva-nature; this is taught in the Yogasañcāra by the Supreme Lord.

tal-a15-v392 — In the fourteenfold range—in being, in flower, incense, food offering, lamp, recitation, and likewise in fire offering—Caṇḍikā is present everywhere.

tal-a15-v393 — She offers into the fire, recites, worships, laughs, and moves; in eating and in sexual union, that same goddess, dwelling in the body, is the doer of all actions.

tal-a15-v394 — But for those who do not rest in that firmly established awareness, a gradual method is taught so that this realization may be accomplished.

tal-a15-v395 — One should clearly remember: 'I am Śiva, consisting of mantra; my resolves are of that same nature; and the fire-pit, fire, and so on arise from Śiva.'

tal-a15-v396 — For this very reason, even there, depending on whether firmness or lack of firmness is seen—while the rite is being performed or after it is done—the consecration may be slight or great.

tal-a15-v397 — Just as someone lacking natural insight may at first grasp a sentence through skill gained by practice, so too it should be understood in this case.

tal-a15-v398 — Drawing, sprinkling, striking, smearing, tracing the axle-circle, and the consecrations of spreading, enclosure, and seat—these ten acts pertaining to the fire-pit are performed by means of the weapon-mantra.

tal-a15-v399 — The center is taken with two darbha blades, the walls are covered with kuśa, upward lines are drawn on the three east-facing lines, and kuśa is also placed outside.

tal-a15-v400 — The auspicious creeper and the square are made, the worship and seat of the ten world-lords are arranged, and the fire-pit is established and protected by the power of the weapon-fire.
