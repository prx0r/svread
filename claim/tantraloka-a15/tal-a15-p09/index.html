tal-a15-v81 — Those outer places are for particular attainments, not for liberation. The inner mountain-top and the like are within the body, approached through the application of prāṇa.

tal-a15-v82 — For practitioners this is a means to attainment, not to liberation. The pīṭha-place, however, is always fit for worship, as the scriptures declare.

tal-a15-v83 — That pīṭha, in its outer and inner form, is clearly present outside and in the body, because the Supreme Lord set it forth in the revered Naiśa Sañcāra.

tal-a15-v84 — His will is the supporting pīṭha in which all that moves and does not move is situated. That foremost pīṭha is Kāmarūpa, and from it comes the pair of bindu and nāda.

tal-a15-v85 — The nāda-pīṭha is Pūrṇagiri on the right, and on the left is the pīṭha Uḍḍayana. With bindu as the chief, this is the triad of pīṭhas.

tal-a15-v86 — One should know the half-pīṭha as having the form of conceptualization. Beyond that, in the śākta domain, Kuṇḍalinī and Vedakala belong to the triad of upapīṭhas.

tal-a15-v87 — Devīkoṭṭa and Ujjayinī are two, and Kulagiri is another; Lālana, Baindava, and Vyāpti form the triad of saṃdohakas.

tal-a15-v88 — With Puṇḍravardhana, Vārendra, and likewise Ekāmra on the outside, the pīṭha is declared ninefold in an inner-outer sequence.

tal-a15-v89 — Those who know the kṣetras speak of an eightfold group corresponding to the eight petals of the heart-lotus: Prayāga, Varaṇā, then Aṭṭahāsa, and Jayantikā.

tal-a15-v90 — Vārāṇasī, Kāliṅga, Kulūtā, and likewise Lāhulā belong to the eight upakṣetras, which they say correspond to the eight front petals of the heart-lotus.
