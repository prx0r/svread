## Reading (machine draft, verse-mapped)

**tal-a15-v21** — And it is said in the Svacchanda scripture that the teacher himself should ask the disciple: what fruit do you seek? I will begin the means suited to that.
**tal-a15-v22** — Because inclinations differ, attainment of the goal is impelled by mantra. In the offering, mantra, seal, path, and substances have a common use.
**tal-a15-v23** — Because inclinations differ, the fruits of disciples and of the teacher differ. The Shaiva practitioner is twofold: one whose way of life abandons worldly concerns.
**tal-a15-v24** — One type follows worldly duty, desires fruit, abides in what is good, and avoids what is evil. The seeker of liberation is twofold: the seedless one is free from pledges and the like.
**tal-a15-v25** — Another kind of candidate is like a child, a fool, an old person, a woman, an enjoyer, or someone sick. For such a person, the teaching of Shiva prescribes the seeded initiation.
**tal-a15-v26** — But for those who can endure the pairs of opposites, there is a seeded initiation made of pledges that confers grace. They must observe it, and they must also keep the special pledges.
**tal-a15-v27** — For a desireless seeker of liberation, one should clearly contemplate the non-existence of past and future actions. But one should not do anything to remove the action that has already begun.
**tal-a15-v28** — But for a practitioner who seeks power, one should purify in just that way. This initiation, grounded in Shiva-duty, takes away worldly duty.
**tal-a15-v29** — For those who remain in worldly duty, this initiation purifies only what is unrighteous, not what is auspicious. It is an initiation without mantra worship.
**tal-a15-v30** — When the body formed by already-begun karma falls away, this person enjoys powers such as aṇimā. After enjoying them, he goes onward to the level where he becomes joined, whether in the sakala state or in a limited condition.
