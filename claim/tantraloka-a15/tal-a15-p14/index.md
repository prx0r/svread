## Reading (machine draft, verse-mapped)

**tal-a15-v131** — She is like a mother because she brings forth what is to come, and so she is called the mother. As Malini, garlanded and upheld by the Rudras, she supports both accomplishment and liberation.
**tal-a15-v132** — She flowers in fruits and is worthy of worship, like a six-footed bee in the sound of withdrawal. Because of ra and la, she is endowed with powers of withdrawal, giving, taking, and more.
**tal-a15-v133** — Śambhunātha declared that this sound-array Mālinī is of the nature of Śiva and Śakti, remembered as one.
**tal-a15-v134** — Because each is full by Śiva-Śakti nature, this restores what is lost in rite, power, or form.
**tal-a15-v135** — Mantras that are placed are filled by replacement and give that fruit, as said in Śrīpūrvatantra, even without special rite.
**tal-a15-v136** — For the purpose of the desire-active body, Mālinī is placed; this qualifier is determined as cause and cause-meaning, though the source differs.
**tal-a15-v137** — This was spoken here for accomplishing the desired fruit: even mantras with collyrium, Garuḍa and others, are not supreme.
**tal-a15-v138** — Filled by Mālinī, they accomplish by strength; but for liberation, even one desiring fruit places another mantra here as Mālinī.
**tal-a15-v139** — Place after reciting, and recite without effort for release; thus place Mātṛkā or Mālinī in order, both.
**tal-a15-v140** — According to accomplishment or liberation, letters are placed; both simultaneously use akṣa-hrīṃ and napha-hrīṃ, masses of union and separation.
