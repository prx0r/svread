**tal-a15-v111** — Although attainments and purity of being may both arise, a person whose heart has become pure reflects on whatever is best of all in that situation.

**tal-a15-v115** — Wherever the heart comes to rest in a beautiful stillness, that place is fit for worship; having reached it, a purified practitioner may remain outwardly engaged while grounded there.
**tal-a15-v116** — For the establishment of outer worship, one should perform a general nyāsa using the Mātṛkā, the Mālinī, or both, either in sequence or in non-sequence.
**tal-a15-v117** — Using the two modes of emission and withdrawal, one should place the elements one by one and in pairs on the forehead, face, eyes, ears, nose, cheeks, teeth, and lips.
**tal-a15-v118** — In this stage, sixteen phonemes ending with visarga are placed in pairs, including on the crown-tuft and tongue, while the ka and ca groups are assigned to the right and left shoulders, arms, hands, fingers, and nails.
**tal-a15-v119** — One should place the ṭa and ta groups in order on the hips, thighs, and so forth, and place the pa group on the sides, back, belly, and heart, making a ninefold assignment.
**tal-a15-v120** — This completes the Mātṛkā nyāsa, extending through the bodily constituents from skin to seed, and now the Mālinī nyāsa is explained.
