## Reading (machine draft, verse-mapped)

**tal-a15-v251** — One invokes Yogeśvarī, the supreme, full, steadfast Kālasaṃkarṣiṇī, augmented by the twelve Shaktis of her limbs, faces, and retinue.
**tal-a15-v252** — At the time of installation, the guru should contemplate according to the aim and practice of the rite. The supreme goddess, abiding in the middle of the triad, is the non-differentiating one.
**tal-a15-v253** — She is proclaimed as the being of unlimited consciousness alone. This is declared in the Sāra scripture and in the Yāmala, and therefore this is how the goddess is understood.
**tal-a15-v254** — The form with faces, the Shakti, the triad of Shaktis, the group of eight, the five limbs, and the supreme Shakti make up the Shakta installation, which is also sixfold.
**tal-a15-v255** — This Yāmala great installation gives the fruits of accomplishment and liberation. But one who seeks liberation alone should first perform the Shakta installation.
**tal-a15-v256** — The gurus say that for one who desires liberation, the pair of installations described should be performed beginning with the feet, in the order of withdrawal.
**tal-a15-v257** — However many divisions are declared as denoting Śambhu, Shakti, and the individual, in all those mantras the installation is proclaimed as sixfold.
**tal-a15-v258** — But the mantra that is invoked, endowed with its limbs, should be the sixth. Thus in all cases this installation is stated as sixfold.
**tal-a15-v259** — Afterward come the display of mudrās by body, mind, and speech. There are the five states beginning with waking, and a sixth called the unsurpassed.
**tal-a15-v260** — Because of the six causes, the sixfold selfhood, and the joining of the thirty-six principles, when the sixfold great installation is performed, this whole universe is powerfully brought into unity.
