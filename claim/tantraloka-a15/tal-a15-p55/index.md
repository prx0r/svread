## Reading (machine draft, verse-mapped)

**tal-a15-v541** — Even if someone stands in the forest like a log, follows the razor-edge vow, and is firmly established in restraints and observances, he still does not attain the supreme state.
**tal-a15-v542** — But one who is devoted to worship of the guru in thought, action, and speech obtains from the pleased guru the full and greatly wondrous highest good.
**tal-a15-v543** — Just as the earth, covered on all sides by falling snow, becomes hard like stone through contact with the wind and remains so, so too a certain state becomes fixed.
**tal-a15-v544** — In restraints and the like, when one becomes immobile, only a limited state is grasped, like that hardened snow; but from a properly worshipped guru one obtains full, expanding knowledge.
**tal-a15-v545** — Consciousness is present everywhere and abides in the knowable; the teaching of one who knows this can immediately lift one upward. Therefore one should worship the guru.
**tal-a15-v546** — The revered Sara says that even conversation with such a guru destroys sin in an instant; therefore one should examine a guru carefully, using scriptural teaching and the signs of true knowledge.
**tal-a15-v547** — Live according to scriptural conduct and associate with the guru in that way. He may sometimes teach out of affection, but he cannot be won over by greed.
**tal-a15-v548** — When the guru is pleased, the gods and ancestors are satisfied; beings cross beyond the hells and go immediately to the great city of Shiva.
**tal-a15-v549** — The house where the guru eats or stays is said to reach Shiva's city; knowing this, one should always worship one's own guru in the ancestral shraddha rite.
**tal-a15-v550** — The guru himself should eat and direct others for that purpose; an initiated disciple should not perform the shraddha and similar rites independently.
