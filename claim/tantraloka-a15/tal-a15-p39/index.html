tal-a15-v381 — After worshipping them with offerings of fragrance, flowers, and so on according to rule and preceded by mantra, the disciple, holding the sword-vessel in hand, pours down a stream.

tal-a15-v382 — The guru, holding the jar in hand, should follow while saying this: 'O Śakra, by you, in your own direction, obstacles must be calmed.'

tal-a15-v383 — One must remain attentive until the end of the rite, by Śiva's command; but in a three-syllable name mostly belonging to Nirṛti, only a single 'bhoḥ' should be used.

tal-a15-v384 — One should remove it because this mantra is spoken as bound by meter; then that jar should be placed in the Īśāna direction upon the scattered offerings.

tal-a15-v385 — Now the weapon-vessel should be placed in the south, in relation to the pot, and the group of mantras, strengthened by absorption in the pot's water, is to be used.

tal-a15-v386 — Meditating on the mantra-group as pure light, he should nourish the whole rite; therefore the guru should worship the entire assembly of mantras in the pot.

tal-a15-v387 — Using the previous method, he should employ the weapon-mantra in the karkarī vessel to drive away obstacles; then the guru should worship in the middle of the house on a fragrant diagram.

tal-a15-v388 — He may worship the mantra-deity as the triad, or in a yāmala form, or as a single unity; then, for the fire rite, he should prepare the fire-pit.

tal-a15-v389 — Through contemplation, the fire-pit—arising in an unprecedented way from resolve and the recitation of pure mantras—is understood to be Śiva's power of action.

tal-a15-v390 — Indeed, the supreme consecration can occur even without other ritual sequences, whether in the body, on the altar-ground, in the liṅga, in a vessel, or in water or fire.
