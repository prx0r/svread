## Reading (machine draft, verse-mapped)

**tal-a08-v171** — The mass of reality is said to partake of the collectivity of Shiva and Shakti, and because it turns toward manifestation it is called an egg.
**tal-a08-v172** — One might object that powers immersed in Shiva should also be eggs, but they are not, because they have not fallen from their own state of power.
**tal-a08-v173** — Another clarifying term is used so that egg-ness is not wrongly extended to bodies and sense bases, because nothing is inserted into them in that way.
**tal-a08-v174** — If bodies and sense bases form a collectivity, their oneness is explained by the word 'undivided', which shows that they share the same kind.
**tal-a08-v175** — Someone might think that, even without using the term 'mass of reality', each principle by itself could have the nature of an egg; but the text challenges that idea.
**tal-a08-v176** — Egg-nature would wrongly extend to a principle made only of qualities, subtle elements, and gross elements; but the word 'reality' here means the whole made of bodies, sense bases, and worlds.
**tal-a08-v177** — Because that form has already been defined, its collectivity is called an egg; and this collectivity belongs to the Lord who bears the universe as his body.
**tal-a08-v178** — For that reason, other distinguishing terms were introduced, to show that egg-ness applies only within the limited states of the path-sphere that depends on Maya.
**tal-a08-v179** — Others say the distinguishing terms were used to prevent egg-ness from being misapplied; in any case, beyond the Brahma egg described here there are one hundred Rudras.
**tal-a08-v180** — Over those hundred Rudras stand eleven radiant Rudra-lords of their own: Ananta, Kapalin, Agni, Yama, Nairrita, Bala, and the rest named in sequence.
