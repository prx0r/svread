## Reading (machine draft, verse-mapped)

**tal-a08-v271** — Then, in connection with that attainment, Paśupati, Indra, Upendra, Viriñci, the gods, and also gandharvas, yakṣas, rākṣasas, ancestors, and sages perform varied sacrifices.
**tal-a08-v272** — The supreme equilibrium of the qualities is the unmanifest. Above the level of the qualities are Krodheśa, Caṇḍa, Saṃvarta, Jyotiḥ, Piṅgala, and Sūraka.
**tal-a08-v273** — There are Pañcāntaka, Ekavīra, and Śikhin, making eight. There that principle is the dense resting place of the soul, prakṛti, the root, pradhāna, the unmanifest.
**tal-a08-v274** — These are synonymous names for the cause of the qualities, belonging to what arises from māyā; all the embodied knowers whose minds are stained by innate and adventitious impurity are there.
**tal-a08-v275** — All of them are established there, along with Rudras who enjoy the experiences arising from it. Some have faculties that are deluded, manifest, or dissolved, while others are without faculties.
**tal-a08-v276** — In the teaching, faculties that are not yet governed and cannot act are called deluded, while those that clearly grasp their fixed objects are called unfolded.
**tal-a08-v277** — At the great dissolution they are broken and dissolved; at creation they are not yet produced. Dependent on will, they are again called without faculties, and the same is true of their effects.
**tal-a08-v278** — At the level of the puruṣa principle there are the nine satisfactions, the eight accomplishments, and likewise the eight worlds beginning with aṇimā.
**tal-a08-v279** — When what is not the true principle is taken as a principle, the resulting contentment is called satisfaction. Taking what should be rejected as acceptable is called accomplishment, as the Kāpilas say.
**tal-a08-v280** — Four satisfactions are internal, named prakṛti, acquisition, time, and fortune. Five arise from turning away from objects through gain, protection, association, loss, and obstruction.
