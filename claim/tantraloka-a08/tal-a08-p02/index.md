**tal-a08-v11** — Thus, understanding oneself as having attained Shiva-hood, it was defined in the Svacchanda teaching that this is not knowledge limited to mere procedure.
**tal-a08-v12** — In the Trishiras teaching, awareness is conceived as root, middle, and tip, unfolding as the thirty-six principles, while memory is the construction of difference.
**tal-a08-v13** — The root of awareness is the 'I am' state with unobstructed distinction; it is the state of all principles, present in one's own self without division.
**tal-a08-v14** — The middle of awareness is marked by a certain relation of support and supported, arising through the differentiation of the principles and the standing of each in its own nature.
**tal-a08-v15** — The tip of awareness is clear, waveless, and full of vast bliss; it brings beings, states, bodies, and the rest into oneness with consciousness.
**tal-a08-v16** — Unbroken consciousness is Bhairava, the supreme Lord; and in the revered Deviyayamala it is said that the path is beautiful with the thirty-six principles.
**tal-a08-v17** — By meditating on the sixfold path one can immediately become of the nature of Shiva, even though that Lord is not different from consciousness.
**tal-a08-v18** — For the full Lord there is no real arrangement of above, beginning, middle, or end; nevertheless, such distinctions are used for the sake of those who must understand and for the one who teaches.
**tal-a08-v19** — Conceptions such as middle and so forth arise according to one's own nature; therefore, by the limitation of the knower's conception, they regard the earthly principle as the lowest.
**tal-a08-v20** — That principle, standing in the innermost interval of all and covered by all the envelopes, is the earthly principle; here, within that earthly principle, the arrangement of worlds is described.
