tal-a23-v31 — Having received consecration, that guru should for six months meditate upon and recite all the mantra-methods taught in the tantras, from oneness.

tal-a23-v32 — When one has truly become one with that, power is attained; then bonds are to be cut, and effort must be made to remain established in that identification.

tal-a23-v33 — A subtle line, like moon-crystal, rises from the heart cakra; it is sound-form, tranquil, and goes through the row of cakras.

tal-a23-v34 — Rooted at the twelfth end, that [form] lies in the suṣumnā between the three paths; there, having filled the heart cakra, one should mutter the blazing-bright mantra.

tal-a23-v35 — It resembles a thighlike flood of flame issuing through the holes of eyes and hair, scattered up to a quiet flame-tip, dissolving all ghee.

tal-a23-v36 — When the mind's inner space is satiated by a stream of ghee, mantras thus become givers of liberation; they would be radiant, awakened, and very pure.

tal-a23-v37 — It goes through the root, bulb, navel, heart, throat, and palate; through the half-moon, the end of sound, and the pervading power.

tal-a23-v38 — Where, in the circle of muttering, resting in the pure self and other circle, from all scattered distinction.

tal-a23-v39 — There, in the Tantra named after the Goddess, this vidyā-vow—called the great mantra—is declared for the accomplishment of mantra potency.

tal-a23-v40 — And that identity-only, as stated in the Spanda treatise, having overcome power, the mantras are of the all-knowing, power-possessing one.
