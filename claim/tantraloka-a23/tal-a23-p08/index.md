## Reading (machine draft, verse-mapped)

**tal-a23-v71** — One fallen into downward vision, whose mind is not reassured by it, yet holding the upward command: sin; let him abandon that and become Shiva.
**tal-a23-v72** — Even a minister, though a limb of the king, is ruined if he harms the king; but the reversal is not the same—one should resort to the upward view.
**tal-a23-v73** — In the earlier Śrī-śāstra it was stated by him: as long as it is not extracted by him alone, here the meaning is this much, augmented by prior knowledge.
**tal-a23-v74** — Thus here the guru is indeed the one referred to by that word: one who possesses such practiced knowledge and holds high status.
**tal-a23-v75** — One who has not been uplifted has no welfare; welfare arises through upliftment by the guru, whether this one or another. Therefore the example of the lotus-born and the sun is explained here.
**tal-a23-v76** — The light of the three worlds is one; indeed another light is night-made; knowledge is another for the Trika guru, and another for those dwelling in your nature.
**tal-a23-v77** — Therefore, when one is in the state of a former guru, then one should again resort to that other who is endowed with marks, facing upward.
**tal-a23-v78** — If that state is present, the upward-facing one should not abandon it out of doubt; clearly, doing so would be a fault—one of two possible courses—that destroys authority.
**tal-a23-v79** — Here the fault is not determined as a fault simply because it stands in the world; rather, that one is considered to be of a nature connected with explaining ignorance.
**tal-a23-v80** — Even for the disciple, the chief fault is the unsteady form of such knowledge; other faults arise from it.
