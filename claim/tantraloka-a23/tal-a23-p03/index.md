## Reading (machine draft, verse-mapped)

**tal-a23-v21** — Eunuchs, women, śūdras, and others who desire that are not to be examined in initiation; they should be examined at the time of knowledge.
**tal-a23-v22** — The teacher, rooted in knowledge, is declared to conduct the seven-sacrifice: initiation, explanation, compassion, friendship, reflection on scripture, and unity with Shiva.
**tal-a23-v23** — In the consecration rite, food-giving and related offerings sustain the seven-sacrifice, and karaṇī, khaṭikā, and related items are given to him.
**tal-a23-v24** — All the throng of equipment is to be offered to the wise one; the one consecrated should afterwards honor the teacher with gifts.
**tal-a23-v25** — A teacher without knowledge, having handed over his own authority to a ritual worker, should not perform initiation and authority without doing so again by his command.
**tal-a23-v26** — Thus he should make him hear; even he should bow and greet. Then from that point on, the former teacher is one who has relinquished authority.
**tal-a23-v27** — As he wishes, he may move about in teaching, initiation, and related matters, free from restraint; he is not hindered in acting, because such transmission is like one lamp from another.
**tal-a23-v28** — In the Siddhanta tantra it is said that the lineage of non-authority is hindered: falling while acting, and before that, acting itself would be hindered.
**tal-a23-v29** — But a teacher who gives instruction according to its meaning is not called contemptuous toward liberation from saṃsāra at the time of the rite.
**tal-a23-v30** — A guru endowed with truth should not initiate a disciple out of pride; one who does so would dwell in hell and become diseased in this life.
