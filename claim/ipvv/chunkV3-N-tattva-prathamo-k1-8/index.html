Now Abhinavagupta gathers it into a brief collection, the tattvārthasaṅgraha, in fifteen verses, so that the student can hold it in mind and mediate upon it. One verse states the supreme essence; nine give the bond of the knower and the known; five give the liberation, which is the recognition. And it all begins with the single great claim. The own-self alone of all beings is the one Lord, the maheśvara. He is the world-form, and his re-recollection — "I, this" — is un-broken. This is not a doctrine; it is the discovery of the very flashing of the self. As the teaching says, all beings are Śiva — the self of all beings is Śiva — and the self-flashing in each is the Lord. So the first word of the collection is: you are that one. But then how does the many arise? In the own-self, in the one Lord, by his freedom, a "this-part" is created, preceded by a contraction. In its midst, the intellect, the breath, the body — known as "this" — and by the this-ness of that distinct-known, an "I-being" shines, the grasper. He who says "I am happy," who grasps himself as this contracted being — that is the bound one. And his liberation is precisely the recognition of what he truly is. The bound one is "the many person." For truly, nothing is bound: in truth there is only the one free Śiva, consciousness-natured. But when consciousness contracts and manifests itself as this finite body and this passing breath, there arises the un-cognition of its own complete form — and with that, the many persons, each caught in the round of action and enjoyment, which appears to him as pleasure and as suffering. The one becomes the many; the free becomes the bound; and all through the simple forgetting of what one is. The Lord, however, remains whole. In the Lord, everywhere, the manifestation and the reflexive-awareness are the knowledge and the action; and the māyā-power, which opens up the "this-ness" of things, is only the covering that lets the world appear. But in the bound one, in the state of self-forgetting, these very powers appear perverted: the pure manifestation becomes the "being-ness," the rajas of activity, the darkness of inertia. So the qualities of the world — the sattva, rajas, tamas of the Sāṃkhya — are nothing but the Lord's own powers, seen in the mirror of the bound soul.
