chunk-b-eligibility-gita-v2 — Dyczkowski-mode.* **The homage that is two-resting, and who is worthy to make it** The work opens with a prostration — and Abhinavagupta takes care to say what that prostration is. It is not a bending of the body, nor an empty sound. It is a *two-resting* homage: it rests at once in the Lord, whom it exalts, and in the devotee, whom it subordinates. The words of homage — "jaya," "victory," "namaḥ," "I bow," "I worship" — all move by one of these two directions, but each must culminate in the other. The "victory" of the Lord, to be real, requires the devotee's self-subordination; the "I bow" of the devotee, to be real, must rest in the Lord's excess. The two — the exalting and the subordinating — are one act, two aspects, made manifest by the single word of homage before the whole assembly. And the attaining of the Lord, the commentary tells us, is the attainment of *dāsya* — of servant-ness. And the servant-ness is extremely hard to get: that is what "kathaṃcit," "in some way," means. It is not a title; it is the very heart of the eligibility. And it is fruitful of the greatest good — for in becoming the servant of the Lord one attains the Lord's own excess, and all the fortunes (dāsyalakṣmī) follow from that servant-ness, granted by the Lord's grace. It is this that makes the devotee one who has attained all. The homage, moreover, is made in the mind and the speech — the mind-awareness and the word-essence — never in the inert body. "Even by speech," the text says, and by the "even" we understand "and by the body." But the prostration proper is the offering of the undivided mind-and-speech, which is the consciousness-power itself; and in offering that, the devotee offers the whole world to the Lord. As the teaching says: "he should offer his own self." The physical prostration — the bowing, the eight-limbed salutation — is only the upacāra, the visible mark by which the inner offering is inferred, not the primary act. And so the question of who is worthy. The objection arises: "the eligibility suits the explainer, who has attained; but how does it suit the mere listeners?" Abhinavagupta's answer is that the listener and the explainer are not two separate undertakings: the recognition is one, and the servant-ness is one. It is by this that the Lord's favor is won — the recognition is preceded by, and inferred from, the Lord's favor. And this is where the Gītā's four kinds of devotees come in — the distressed, the seeker of knowledge, the seeker of wealth, and the knower. All of them, in their several gradations, are worthy of the servant-ness and of the recognition. The door is not narrow in the way of the world's ranks; it is narrow only in the way of the heart — for what is required is the self-subordination, the dāsya, and that, once attained, draws the Lord near. - **Preserved:** the right teacher (samyagvyutpādaka); the Buddhists as makers-and-haters (the Kathāmukhatilaka); the unprecedented prostration; the two-resting homage (jaya/self-subordination, namaḥ/Lord's-excess); the jar-shines-and-sees; "kathaṃcit" = the dāsya is hard to get; "mahāphala" = the attainment of all fortunes (dāsyalakṣmī, by the Lord's grace); "vācāpi" = by body too; the homage in mind/speech, not the inert body; the offering of the whole world; "he should offer his own self"; the physical prostration as the upacāra (inferred mark); the "satyam" (obstacle-removal vs. the greater purpose); "adyāpi" (un-broken, by the book); the explainer honored; the recognition preceded by the Lord's favor; the listener-not-separate objection and answer; the four Gītā-devotees all eligible. - **Added (SUPPLIED, recoverable):** "It is not a title; it is the very heart of the eligibility" (states the dāsya); "in offering that, the devotee offers the whole world" (glosses the viśvārpaṇa); "the door is narrow only in the way of the heart" (the eligibility's point).
