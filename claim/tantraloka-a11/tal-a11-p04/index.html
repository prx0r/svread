tal-a11-v31 — But at the end of that touch, awareness, shaped as pure consciousness-space, in which one is established, approaches the supreme self-luminous.

tal-a11-v32 — Therefore from the bindu comes sound; from that comes form; from that comes taste; thus is said of agitation-nature in spanda; but touch is not so.

tal-a11-v33 — The opinion of Caitanya-Maheśa, which Śrī formerly spoke, is a nourishing sustainer, enlightener, purifier, and giver of space.

tal-a11-v34 — By these words dealing with nivṛtti and the rest, one's own body is the five-element rule that has been stated; now the three principles are described.

tal-a11-v35 — The self reaches only up to vijnanakala; beyond that, in the remainder ending in vidyesvara, Shiva may be in three principles or in one principle, and Shiva is supreme.

tal-a11-v36 — These two differences are produced solely by tattva-difference; thus, in the tattva-adhvan there is loss, but there is no loss from the establishment of the six adhvans.

tal-a11-v37 — Nature, puruṣa, time, māyā, vidyeśa, sauśiva, and Śiva are principles; even in the nine-principle rule, there is the form of the essence-sacrifice.

tal-a11-v38 — Thus, in the procedure called eighteen, the wise would state the law: wherever there is desire for enjoyment, it is applied according to its predominance and usefulness.

tal-a11-v39 — And from other inner contemplation, initiation has endless distinctions; by that, from thirty-six until the rite of the single principle becomes.

tal-a11-v40 — This truth-path was indeed spoken by God, in detail and in brief; this one truth-rule is for the well-awakened guru.
