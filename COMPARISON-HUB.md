# Translation comparison pilot

The reader's `?view=compare` catalog links to translators' own publications. `?view=compare&work=anuttarastika` adds an eight-verse comparison of the existing reader's L2 and a newly prepared English research draft. The new draft, notes, and source alignments are **machine-proposed and await independent Sanskrit review**; they are not a scholar-certified gold translation. The external translators' words are not republished in this repository. Personal comparison notes are stored only in the browser and are scoped to the exact bundled projection hash.

Preserve the differences as editorial questions, not automatic corrections.

## How to reach a genuine reference edition

1. Obtain and identify a suitable source edition and its rights; distinguish digitisation, print edition and manuscript witness. Confirm exact verse boundaries, characters, variants and offsets against that source.
2. Have two Sanskrit readers independently examine every draft verse, morphological decision, disputed compound and textual variant. Record disagreements, their reasons, the particular edition and the reviewers' exact decisions. Verses 4, 6 and 7 especially need independent review.
3. Bind the approved source and translation to immutable revision and full-byte digests. Keep the original candidate and review history; do not promote a `PASS` or a matching hash into a human approval.
4. Track rights and source lineage for each external translation separately. Make in-app side-by-side display conditional on explicit permission or an appropriate licence; keep link-only sources as links. Review each proposed source's scope and verse numbering before automated alignment.
5. Connect the editor-approved revision to the Scholar workflow and author sign-off; then use that exact revision in public reader, essays, teacher and downstream book/audio/video releases. A machine draft may be useful without being labelled gold.

At the inspected svread base `8f19bcccba197766b84d7cd9946cf1072bd64a61`, the earlier inaccurate bibliography count was changed to direct passage references and the formal exporter now labels unbound entries REGISTERED. These are meaningful repairs; a reference still does not establish source-to-theorem entailment, and a matching trace still does not establish human approval.

**P1 — a T-tier essay span need not appear in its source (fix proposed).** At the inspected trika-x base, `formal-trika/tools/essay_check.py` verifies that `span_sha256` matches the supplied `span`, an edition label is nonempty, and the chunk locator exists. It does not compare that span to the bytes of the named passage or verify the edition label. Reproduced with a temporary ledger containing `real source text` and a T-tier claim containing the different string `invented quotation`: a correctly self-hashed invented span, arbitrary edition label, matching claim marker and matching receipt passed `check_slug` with no failures. This demonstrates a gap in the **ready-for-human-review** gate; it does not demonstrate publication of the fabricated claim. [trika-x PR #9](https://github.com/prx0r/trika-x/pull/9) proposes requiring the exact span, source-passage digest and ledger edition in the frozen receipt, with tests for invented text and changed data. It binds to a local ledger, not an independently authenticated edition: edition-to-witness mapping, unique byte offsets, repository revision pinning and a human source/meaning audit remain open.

This change renames the bibliography's `formalized` filter and heading to describe registry tracking and direct passage references rather than implying a proven formalization. The bibliography contains separate candidate and internal-work identities for Anuttarāṣṭikā; do not auto-merge them based only on title. The Pratyabhijñāhṛdaya ledger's multi-sūtra span issue remains in translation-work, despite the reader's separately produced 20-row projection.

## Next useful extensions

* A verse-level edition switch with explicit variant notes, stable edition-scoped locators and a visible uncertainty state.
* Scholar-approved side-by-side translations where permission allows; private imported notes should remain explicitly local or be account-synced only by opt-in.
* A source-first evidence inspector: click a word, see morphology and alternative interpretations, then the exact support for an essay or formal premise.
* A library-wide translation index with separate metadata for translation, commentary, source edition, audio, licence, completeness and language—never assume an index page supplies every verse.
* A cross-tradition terminology map that keeps Śaiva, Buddhist and Vedānta meanings distinct while showing sourced comparisons.
