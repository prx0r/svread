namaste viśvamūrtaye — Homage to You

Abhinavagupta, Mahopadeśaviṃśatikā vv.1–2; translation-work mahopadeshavimshatika · Mahopadeśa mpa-p01-v01 (+ v02 namo namaḥ)

content l02-namaste

1 · Original witness

प्रपञ्चोत्तीर्णरूपाय नमस्ते विश्वमूर्तये । सदादिव्यप्रकाशाय स्वात्मनेऽनन्तशक्तये ॥
prapañcottīrṇarūpāya namaste viśvamūrtaye | sadādivyaprakāśāya svātmane'nantaśaktaye ||

Homage to the world-formed, past the world — to ever-shining divinity, to self of unending power.

  • author: Abhinavagupta
  • work: Mahopadeśaviṃśatikā
  • verse: 1
  • edition: translation-work mahopadeshavimshatika (gold)
  • variants: Companion drill from v.2: namo namaḥ before n- contexts. L2/L3 as placed.

7 · Syntax

  • case roles: datives -āya/-ye “to X”; namas indeclinable; te dative
  • agreement: dat.sg.f -ye; dat.sg.m/n -āya
  • finite verbs: none — homage frame
  • implied: “(I offer) homage to you”

8 · Translation from the syntax

Interlinear: beyond-world-formed-to · homage-to-you · world-formed-to · ever-divine-shining-to · self-unending-power-to

Literal: Homage to the one whose form is beyond the world; to the world-formed, to the ever-divine-shining, to the self of unending power.

Readable: Homage to the world-formed, past the world — to ever-shining divinity, to self of unending power.

Alternatives: namas+te standard; stick to layer citation.

Recognise it — Trika exegesis

Text / Abhinavagupta: Mahopadeśa opens addressing the one beyond prapañca yet world-formed — homage without a separate devotee object.

Bridge: Dative can mark addressee while Trika reads addressee as not-other than homage’s subject.

  • namaste (dat. te) — homage whose target is not second
  • viśvamūrti — world as form of the one addressed
  • ananta-śakti — unending power as self-description

Lakshmanjoo: Not primary here.

Our synthesis: Pedagogical framing only; L3 from translation-work.

10 · Practice and mastery

  • recite: Recite four datives, one per breath.
  • write: Column namas / namaste / namo namaḥ; mark joints.
  • memorise: Memorise dative -āya/-ye as “to …” first.
  • when: Opening homage before study.

Transfer exercises:

  • Reverse namaste.
  • Why namo before namaḥ?
  • Point out one dat.f and one dat.m.
