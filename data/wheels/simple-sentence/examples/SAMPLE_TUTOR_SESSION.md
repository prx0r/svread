# Demonstration: natural tutor flow

Tutor: “Teacher.”
Learner: “गुरुः”
Tutor: “Correct. Next: Student.”
Learner: “शिष्यः”
Tutor: “Correct. Next: Water.”
Learner: “जल”
Tutor: “The stem is जल, but the nominative singular answer is **जलम्**. Next: Ashram.”
Learner: “आश्रमः”
Tutor: “Correct. Next: Book.”

... After diagnostic mastery ...

Tutor: “The student reads a book.”
Learner: “शिष्यः पुस्तकं पठति।”
Tutor: “Correct. Next: The teacher speaks Sanskrit.”
Learner: “गुरुः संस्कृतं वदति।”
Tutor: “Correct. Next: Yesterday I drank water.”
Learner: “ह्यः अहं जलं पिबामि।”
Tutor: “Tense: पिबामि is present. Yesterday in this exercise uses imperfect **अपिबम्**: ह्यः अहं जलम् अपिबम्। Next: Yesterday the student drank tea.”

... At the capstone ...

Tutor: “Yesterday, in the ashram, the teacher gave the student water from his own cup.”
Learner: “ह्यः आश्रमे गुरुः स्वपात्रात् शिष्याय जलम् अददात्।”
Tutor: “Correct: locative आश्रमे; ablative स्वपात्रात्; dative शिष्याय; accusative जलम्; imperfect अददात्. Next: To whom will the teacher give the book tomorrow?”

## Corrections
A learner may omit an explicit pronoun if the verb ending already encodes it; this is usually acceptable. Accept correct word-order alternatives and legitimate external sandhi. Do not mark English-specific word order mandatory. Differentiate a missing word the prompt requires from permissible pro-drop.
